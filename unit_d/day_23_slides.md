### Slide 8: Freytag's Pyramid
**Freytag's Pyramid — a storytelling framework for case studies**

1. **Exposition** — Who has a problem? Why do *you* care? What's the inciting incident?
2. **Rising Action** — What did you discover through research? What challenges emerged?
3. **Climax** — What was the turning point — the test or insight that changed everything?
4. **Falling Action** — How did you refine and iterate based on that insight?
5. **Resolution** — What did you build? What were the outcomes? What did you learn?

The key question for every section: "What did I *learn*?" — not "What did I *make*?"

---

### Slide 9: The Inciting Incident for School Projects
**Your inciting incident is the moment the problem became personally real**

This is NOT "my professor assigned it." It's the moment you became genuinely invested.

- **Personal friction:** "I kept abandoning every habit tracker after a week because..."
- **User interview surprise:** "During my Mom Test interviews, one person said something that reframed the whole problem..."
- **Competitive gap:** "I tried every app in this space and none of them solved..."
- **Observation:** "I watched my roommate spend 20 minutes doing something that should take 2..."

> Be honest that it started as a class project — then show the moment it became real.

---

### Slide 10: Mapping to the Pyramid
**Your case study sections map to the five stages**

**Exposition:** Title page, creative brief, persona, your motivation
*Who is this for and why should we care?*

**Rising Action:** User research, competitive analysis, wireframes, paper prototypes
*What problems did you uncover? What was the landscape?*

**Climax:** The pivotal usability test or insight — before/after comparisons
*What changed your direction?*

**Falling Action:** Style guide, high-fi iteration, testing round 2
*How did you respond to what you learned?*

**Resolution:** Final screens, motion, outcomes, reflection, AI usage
*The polished solution + honest reflection*

---

### Slide 11: Find Your Climax
**Every case study needs a turning point**

The climax is the moment that proves you can *think*, not just execute.

- "I tested it and discovered X, so I changed Y" — the most powerful sentence in a case study
- Before/after comparisons with annotations explaining *why* you changed it are gold
- No climax = everything presented as smooth and linear. It never is.

**Common mistakes:**
- Screenshot dump with no narrative thread
- Skipping straight to the final screens
- Hiding the failures (the most interesting part!)

### Slide 13: The Document Is a Design Artifact
**Your case study should demonstrate your design skills**

Apply the same craft principles from Day 21 to the document itself:

- **Title:** Large, bold — project name and one-line description
- **Section heads:** Clear, consistent — guide the reader through the narrative
- **Body text:** Readable, well-spaced — the bulk of your story
- **Captions:** Small, paired with images — explain what the reader is seeing

Consistent page layout: margins, columns, image placement, whitespace. No "Lorem ipsum."
