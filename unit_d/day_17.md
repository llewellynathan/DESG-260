# Day 17 — Monday, Mar 9
## Unit D: Neue App (Capstone)

**Focus:** Your user flow is a prompt — AI turns your thinking into working code.

**Why This Matters:** You've done the research. You know your competitors. Now it's time to see your app come to life. Today you'll learn to translate competitive insights into a differentiation strategy, map your core experience as a user flow, sketch quick paper wireframes, and then use AI tools to generate a working coded prototype. This isn't about AI replacing your design thinking — it's about AI accelerating your ability to test ideas. A clear user flow and annotated sketches become powerful prompts that help AI understand your intent.

**Targeted Learning Outcomes:**
- Design Strategy
- Better Interactions & Experiences
- Problem Discovery & Research (Curiosity, Empathy, Humility)

**Essential Questions:**

- EQ2: What does a user flow capture that a feature list doesn't?

**Understandings:**

**Students Will Be Able To:**

### Activities

1. **Warm-Up: Competitive Insight Share** (10 min) `[EQ1, U1, S1]`
   - Find a partner (someone NOT in your problem space)
   - Each person shares (3 min each):
     - "The one insight from my competitive analysis that will most influence my design is..."
     - "My app will be different because..."
   - Partner asks one probing question
   - Goal: Articulate your differentiation out loud — it makes it clearer in your own head

2. **Today's Learning Goals** (5 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, EQ3, U1, U2, U3]`
   - **Why this matters:** You've done the research. Today your app comes to life.
   - Preview:
     - Synthesis: Competitive findings → your unique position
     - User flows: Mapping the core experience (this becomes your AI prompt)
     - Quick sketches: Fast thinking tool that guides AI
     - AI prototyping: See your app as working code
   - Connection to forming/rendering: We're at the pivot point — shifting from *forming intent* to *rendering intent*. Your user flow is the bridge, and AI accelerates the rendering.

3. **Direct Instruction: From Research to Prototype** (40 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, EQ3, U1, U2, U3, K1, K2, K3, K4, K5, S1, S2]`

   **Part A: Synthesizing Competitive Findings** (8 min)
   - **I do:** The "So What?" question
     - You have a positioning matrix and four competitor analyses — now what?
     - Every competitive insight should answer: "So what does this mean for MY design?"
     - Example: "All four competitors have cluttered dashboards" → "My app will have a focused, single-task home screen"
   - **I do:** Finding your gap
     - Look at your positioning matrix — where is the empty space?
     - Look at your competitor weaknesses — what pain points can you solve better?
     - Your differentiation strategy = gap in the market + something you can actually execute

   **Part B: User Flow as AI Prompt** (12 min)
   - **I do:** What is a user flow?
     - A user flow maps *how someone moves through your app* to accomplish a goal
     - Components: entry point → core screens (3-4) → decision points → success state
     - Start with the "happy path" — the ideal case where everything works
   - **I do:** Your flow is your prompt
     - AI prototyping tools need to understand your intent
     - A clear, annotated user flow tells AI: "Here's what I want the user to experience"
     - Messy flow = messy AI output; clear flow = useful AI output
   - **I do:** What makes a good user flow for AI?
     - **Specific screen names:** "Home Dashboard" not "Screen 1"
     - **Clear actions:** "User taps 'Start Workout'" not "User proceeds"
     - **Annotations:** Brief notes on what each screen should show/do
     - **Success criteria:** What does "done" look like?

   **Part C: Sketches as Thinking Tools** (8 min)
   - **I do:** Why we still sketch
     - Quick paper sketches (15-20 min) help you think through layout and hierarchy
     - Sketching is a thinking tool — faster than words for spatial problems
     - Sketch at phone scale, include real content, mark tap targets
   - **I do:** Annotate your sketches
     - Label key elements (not just boxes)
     - Mark tap targets with what happens ("→ Workout Selection")
     - Add brief notes on functionality
     - These annotations make your sketches useful as AI input

   **Part D: Combining Your Artifacts for AI** (12 min)
   - **I do:** Traditional artifacts, new application
     - User flow diagrams and sketches are traditional design skills — your professors will require them
     - AI prompting is a *new skill*: combining these artifacts with the right context
   - **I do:** What each artifact tells AI
     - **User flow diagram:** Screen sequence, navigation logic, decision points
     - **Annotated sketches:** Layout, visual hierarchy, UI elements, tap targets
     - **Text prompt:** Everything NOT in the visuals — purpose, style, tech stack
   - **I do:** The synthesis process
     - **Visual inputs** (upload directly): User flow diagram + annotated sketch photos
     - **Text prompt** (contextual glue): What the visuals don't show
       - App description and purpose
       - Target user context
       - Style direction (colors, mood, references)
       - Technical requirements (React, mobile-first, etc.)
   - **I do:** Example prompt structure
     ```
     Build a fitness tracking app in React. Target user is a busy
     professional who wants quick 15-minute workouts.

     [Attach: user flow diagram]
     [Attach: annotated sketches]

     Style: Clean, minimal, dark mode. Rounded corners,
     lots of whitespace. Energetic but not aggressive.
     ```
   - **I do:** AI prototyping tools
     - **Figma Make:** Already in Figma, easiest access, good for quick UI generation
     - **V0 (Vercel):** Best for React/Next.js, strong UI generation, iterative
     - **Lovable:** Full-stack apps, good for MVPs, deploys instantly
     - **Bolt.new:** Quick prototypes, simple interactions
   - **I do:** Demo — From artifacts to code
     - Show a user flow diagram and annotated sketches
     - Write the contextual text prompt (what's NOT in the visuals)
     - Upload everything together, show AI output
   - **We do:** What context is missing?
     - Show a flow + sketches — class identifies: What would you add in the text prompt?
     - Discuss: What can AI see vs. what do you need to tell it?

4. **D4 Assignment Introduction** (5 min) `[EQ2, EQ3, U2, U3, K2, K3, S2, S3, S4]`
   - **D4: User Flow + Sketches + AI Prototype** (due Wed, Mar 11 @ 5:15pm)
   - **User Flow Diagram:**
     - Core function in 3-4 screens (not counting onboarding)
     - Entry point → main screens → decision points → success state
     - Annotations explaining what each screen does
   - **Quick Sketches:**
     - Paper sketches of your main screens (5-6 screens)
     - To scale, with real content and tap targets marked
     - These are process documentation, not polished designs
   - **AI Prototype:**
     - Working coded prototype from V0, Lovable, or similar tool
     - Should demonstrate your core user flow
     - Include link to deployed prototype
   - **Bring to class Wednesday** — you'll test your AI prototype with classmates

5. **Work Session: Flow → Sketch → Prompt → Prototype** (60 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, EQ3, U1, U2, U3, K1, K2, K3, K4, K5, S1, S2, S3, S4]`
   - **First 10 min:** Write your differentiation strategy
     - Review your D3 competitive analysis
     - Write 2-3 sentences: What is your app's unique position? How will it be different?
   - **Next 12 min:** Create your user flow diagram
     - Map your core function: entry → 3-4 screens → success
     - Be specific: screen names, user actions, what's displayed
     - This is a traditional artifact — do it well
   - **Next 12 min:** Quick annotated sketches
     - Sketch your main screens at phone scale
     - Annotate: label elements, mark tap targets with destinations
     - Include real content — these annotations help AI understand intent
   - **Next 6 min:** Write your contextual prompt
     - Photograph your flow diagram and sketches
     - Write the context AI can't see: app purpose, target user, style direction, tech stack
     - Don't describe what's in the images — add what's NOT visible
   - **Remaining 20 min:** AI prototyping
     - Open V0, Lovable, or your preferred tool
     - Upload visuals + contextual prompt together
     - Iterate: refine based on what AI produces
   - Instructor circulates for 1:1 feedback on:
     - Is your user flow specific enough to guide AI?
     - Are your sketch annotations clear?
     - Does your text prompt add context the visuals don't show?

**Total: 120 min**
