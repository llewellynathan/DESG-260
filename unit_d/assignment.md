# Assignment 4: Neue Application

## "Inform and Delight"

Every great product does two things at once: it **informs** — communicating clearly, helping users accomplish their goals — and it **delights** — creating moments of surprise, satisfaction, and emotional connection. The tension between these isn't a problem to solve; it's a creative opportunity to embrace.

For this final project, you'll venture into custom-made multi-screen design for multiple interfaces. You'll build upon everything you've learned to create your own brand-new interactive product.


## ✏️ The Assignment

Design a brand-new digital product that helps people with some aspect of personal improvement. You'll take it through the entire design process: ideation, validation, research, prototyping, testing, visual identity development, high-fidelity design, and documentation.

Any new digital product succeeds only when it can convince people to use it instead of something else. Your research will need to demonstrate how your product will better serve the intended audience. That doesn't mean every part of your digital product will be completely new — you'll build upon what competitors do well while improving where they fall short.


### PRODUCT FOCUS

💚 Your app should involve some aspect of **personal improvement** within one of these categories:
   - Education (learning, skill-building, study tools)
   - Productivity (habits, time management, organization)
   - Health/Fitness (exercise, nutrition, mental wellness, sleep)
   - Finance (budgeting, saving, financial literacy)

💚 Primary function must be clear and achievable within 3-4 core screens (not counting onboarding)

💚 Design for at least **two platforms** — mobile phone primary, plus:
   - Tablet
   - Watch
   - Desktop
   - Glasses/Vision Pro
   - Car dashboard
   - Or another peripheral device (fitness band, thermostat, speaker, etc.)

❌ **Cannot be:** a dating app, e-commerce/shopping platform, or social media app (limited social sharing features are acceptable)

❌ **Cannot rely primarily on:** content consumption (news, entertainment) unless tied directly to learning outcomes


### SCOPE & DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

💚 Design the core function from onboarding to success screen
💚 Adhere to platform conventions (Apple HIG or Material Design) — deviate deliberately and with good reason
💚 Custom app logo and wordmark (branding is not the focus, but must be intentional)
💚 Custom typography hierarchy — no default system fonts
💚 At least 2 custom microinteractions/animations demonstrating motion design
💚 Multiple rounds of user research and usability testing (minimum 10 user interviews total)
❌ Do not design for a problem you haven't validated with real users
❌ Do not skip paper prototyping — start low-fidelity before going digital


### AI USAGE POLICY

AI tools (V0, Lovable, Bolt.new, Figma Make, etc.) are powerful **validation tools**. Use them to quickly test whether your concept works — generate a coded prototype, put it in front of users, and learn fast.

However, your AI prototype is a **disposable artifact**, not your final design. All high-fidelity screens (D8, D9) must be **hand-built in Figma from scratch**. You may reference the AI output for layout ideas, but your final design should reflect your own intentional decisions about spacing, hierarchy, typography, color, and interaction patterns.

**Why?** The AI can render a plausible interface, but it can't form intent. Your job is to demonstrate that you can do both — discover the right problem (forming) AND craft a beautiful, functional solution (rendering). If the AI does the rendering for you, you've only done half the work.

## 🚃 Process

The deliverables below span Days 15-26 (approximately 6 weeks). Each builds on the previous, culminating in a comprehensive case study.


### D1: Ideation & Initial Ideas
**Due: Day 16 (Wed, Mar 4) — come to class with 3 app ideas**

Generate as many ideas as possible (mind maps, lists, post-its). Narrow to your top 3 ideas, with one clear favorite.

**For each idea, write a Problem/Opportunity Statement using one of these formats:**

**Opportunity Statement:**
> "I want to help [target audience] who want to [user need] by [avoiding current pain point] and [improving value added] unlike [current competitors]"

**Lean UX Problem Statement:**
> "[Our product] was designed to achieve [goals]. We have observed that it isn't meeting [these goals], which is causing [adverse effect]. How might we improve it so users are more successful based on [measurable criteria]?"

**Lean UX Hypothesis:**
> "We believe that [doing this] for [these people] will achieve [this outcome]. We will know this is true when we see [this signal/metric]."

**What to bring:**
- 3 app ideas (one favorite among them)
- Problem/Opportunity Statement for each idea
- Brief explanation of why each matters to real people

---

### D2: Idea Validation & Competitive Analysis
**Due: Day 18 (Wed, Mar 11) — submit to Learning Suite**

Validate your chosen idea through research before committing.

**Competitive Analysis:**
- Top-level analysis of 4 competing products (any mix of physical/digital)
- 2-paragraph summary of who's in the space and what they're doing
- Competitive Analysis Matrix (quadrant positioning chart)
- Validate choices with evidence of market share, audience usage

**Validation Research:**
- Talk to 4+ potential users using "Mom Test" style interviews
- Document key insights — what did you learn that surprised you?

Submit: PDF with competitive analysis matrix and interview insights

---

### D3: Persona, Journey Map, Job Stories & User Flow
**Due: Day 19 (Mon, Mar 16) — submit to Learning Suite**

Translate research into design artifacts.

**User Persona (1 page):**
- Name, age, photo
- Interests and relevant behaviors
- Brands they like
- How they currently solve (or don't solve) the problem
- Market demographic segment

**Job Stories (2-3 per persona):**
Write job stories that capture the context, motivation, and desired outcome:
> "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]."

Job stories help you understand *why* users need your product, not just *what* features they want.

**Observational/Emotional Journey Map:**
- Map the current experience (before your app exists)
- Include emotional highs and lows
- Identify opportunity moments

**User Flow:**
- How will users move through your app?
- Core function in 3-4 screens (not counting onboarding)
- Design your user flow to guide AI prototyping later (clear, well-annotated)

---

### D4: Brand Audit
**Due: Day 20 (Wed, Mar 18) — submit at beginning of class**

Visual brand audit of your 4 competitors (1 page each):
- Logo
- Top 3 brand colors
- Typography (header + body, with samples in context)
- Icon sample (3-7 icons)
- Illustration/photography samples
- 3 adjectives describing the brand's personality

**Purpose:** Understand how competitors position themselves visually before developing your own identity.

### D5: Paper Prototype + AI Experimentation
**Due: Day 21 (Mon, Mar 23) — bring to class**

Sketch your app before opening Figma.

**Paper Prototype:**
- 5-6 screens for your main feature/flow
- 3-4 screens for secondary features
- To scale (actual phone size)
- Detailed enough for someone to "use" by pointing

**AI Experimentation (in class):**
After paper prototyping, you'll experiment with translating your sketches to digital using AI:
- Photograph your paper prototypes
- Prompt AI tools (V0, Lovable, Figma Make) with your sketches and user flow
- Compare AI output to your original design intent
- Learn what AI needs to produce useful results (clear flows, annotations, context)

This isn't about replacing your design work — it's about learning how to collaborate with AI tools effectively.

---

### D6: Usability Testing Round 1
**Due: Day 21 (Mon, Mar 23) — testing happens in class**

Test your paper prototype with real users.

**Testing Script:**
- Intro
- Contextualizing questions (habits, interests, demographics)
- Prototype introduction
- Task description

**5 Act Method:**
1. Friendly welcome
2. Context questions
3. Introduce the prototype
4. Walk through tasks (observe, don't help)
5. Quick debrief

Document: Voice memo or written notes with distilled insights

## How to Know You Did It Right

1. **Problem validation:** Can you explain why this problem is worth solving, backed by real user research?
2. **User understanding:** Does your persona feel like a real person, grounded in actual interviews?
3. **Visual coherence:** Does your visual identity (logo, type, color, icons) work together to reinforce your product's purpose?
4. **Platform appropriateness:** Would your app feel at home on the intended platform?
5. **Iteration evidence:** Can you point to specific changes you made based on user feedback?
6. **Motion with purpose:** Do your animations enhance usability, not just decoration?
7. **Story clarity:** Does your case study tell a compelling story that someone outside the class could follow?
