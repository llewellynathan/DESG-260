# Day 18 — Wednesday, Mar 11
## Unit D: Neue App (Capstone)

**Focus:** Testing answers two questions: "Can they use it?" and "Should we build it?"

**Why This Matters:** You have a working prototype — but you don't know if it works. Most designers only test usability: "Can users tap the right buttons?" That's necessary but not sufficient. You also need to test solution fit: "Does this actually solve their problem? Would they use this instead of what they do now?" Today you'll learn to plan tests that answer both questions. The difference between a mediocre product and a great one often comes down to asking the right questions during testing.

**Targeted Learning Outcomes:**
- Problem Discovery & Research (Curiosity, Empathy, Humility)
- Communication and Feedback
- Better Interactions & Experiences

---

### Desired Results

**Essential Questions:**
- EQ1: What's the difference between testing usability and validating your solution?
- EQ2: How do you write tasks and questions that reveal truth instead of confirmation?
- EQ3: How do you facilitate a test without leading the participant?

**Understandings:**
- U1: Usability testing reveals whether people CAN use your product; solution validation reveals whether they WOULD
- U2: The quality of your test depends on the quality of your tasks and questions
- U3: Your job as facilitator is to observe and learn, not to explain or defend

**Students Will Know:**
- K1: The distinction between usability testing and solution validation

- K3: The 5 Act Method for structuring a test session
- K4: Common facilitation mistakes and how to avoid them

**Students Will Be Able To:**
- S1: Write realistic tasks that test core functionality
- S2: Write open-ended questions that reveal solution fit
- S3: Facilitate a test session without leading the participant
- S4: Document findings with specific, actionable insights

3. **Direct Instruction: Planning a Great Prototype Test** (35 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, EQ3, U1, U2, U3, K1, K2, K3, K4, S1, S2]`

   **Part A: Two Types of Testing** (10 min)
   - **I do:** The two questions every test should answer
     - **Usability:** "Can they use it?" — Can users accomplish tasks? Where do they get stuck?
     - **Solution validation:** "Should we build it?" — Does this solve a real problem? Would they choose this over alternatives?
   - **I do:** Why you need both
     - A usable product that solves the wrong problem = failure
     - A great solution that's unusable = failure
     - You need signal on BOTH to succeed
   - **I do:** Different questions for different goals
     - Usability questions: "Show me how you would..." / "What do you expect to happen?"
     - Validation questions: "How does this compare to what you do now?" / "Would this change your behavior?"
   - **We do:** Categorize some example questions
     - Show 4-5 questions — class identifies: usability or validation?

   **Part B: Writing Good Tasks and Questions** (12 min)
   - **I do:** What makes a good task?
     - **Realistic:** Based on actual user goals, not feature demonstrations
     - **Specific:** "You want to track a 20-minute run you just completed" not "Use the app"
     - **No hints:** Don't name UI elements — "Log your workout" not "Tap the + button"
   - **I do:** What makes a good question?
     - **Open-ended:** "What would you do next?" not "Would you tap this button?"
     - **Past behavior:** "How do you currently track workouts?" (real) not "Would you use this?" (hypothetical)
     - **Non-leading:** "What do you think of this?" not "Don't you think this is easier?"
   - **I do:** The Mom Test applies here too
     - People will be nice. They'll say they'd use it.
     - Your job is to design questions that reveal truth, not confirm your hopes.
     - **You can test with anyone** — friends, roommates, family — if you ask the right questions.
   - **I do:** Mom Test style questions for prototypes
     - These are validation questions grounded in real behavior, not hypotheticals
     - Examples:
       - "How do you currently handle [this problem]?" (reveals if problem is real)
       - "What happened the last time you tried to [relevant task]?" (reveals pain points)
       - "What would you do if this app didn't exist?" (reveals alternatives and switching cost)
       - "Who else has this problem?" (reveals if they're representative)
     - The key: ask about **past behavior** and **current reality**, not future intentions
   - **We do:** Rewrite bad questions
     - Show 2-3 leading or hypothetical questions
     - Class rewrites them using good question principles

   **Part C: The 5 Act Method + Facilitation** (13 min)
   - **I do:** The 5 Act structure
     1. **Friendly welcome** (1 min) — Make them comfortable, explain think-aloud
     2. **Context questions** (3 min) — Current behavior, relevant habits, background
     3. **Introduce the prototype** (1 min) — "This is an early prototype. Some things won't work."
     4. **Tasks** (10-15 min) — Give tasks one at a time. Observe. Don't help.
     5. **Debrief** (5 min) — Overall impressions, comparison to current solutions, would they use it?
   - **I do:** Facilitation principles
     - **Silence is your friend.** Let them struggle. That's data.
     - **Don't explain.** If they're confused, note it. Don't rescue them.
     - **Don't defend.** If they criticize, say "Tell me more." Not "Well, actually..."
     - **Ask "Why?"** When they do something unexpected, ask what they were thinking.
   - **I do:** Common mistakes
     - Helping too quickly ("Oh, you tap here")
     - Leading questions ("You found that easy, right?")
     - Defending choices ("That's because we wanted to...")
     - Asking hypothetical questions ("Would you use this?")

4. **D5 Assignment Introduction** (5 min) `[S4]`
   - **D5: Usability Testing Round 1** (due Mon, Mar 16 @ 5:15pm)
   - **Test at least 5 people** outside of class before Day 19
   - **Test anyone** — friends, roommates, family, strangers. The skill is asking good questions, not finding "unbiased" testers.
   - **Your test should include:**
     - 4-5 task-based questions (realistic scenarios)
     - At least 1 follow-up question per task (dig into confusion or success)
     - 4-5 Mom Test style questions (past behavior, current reality — not hypotheticals)
   - **Document for each test:**
     - Who they are (relationship to you, relevant background)
     - What happened (observations, not interpretations)
     - Key quotes
     - Usability issues discovered
     - Solution validation signals
   - **Synthesis:**
     - Patterns across tests
     - Top 3 usability issues to fix
     - Top 3 insights about solution fit
     - Reflection: Which questions got the most honest answers? Which seemed to invite polite non-answers?
     - What you'll change in the next iteration
   - Submit: PDF (2 pages: key insights + raw data)

5. **Test Planning Workshop** (20 min) `[EQ2, U2, K2, S1, S2]`
   - **First 10 min:** Write your test plan
     - 4-5 realistic task-based questions for your core flow
     - A follow-up question for each task (to dig deeper)
     - 4-5 Mom Test style questions (past behavior, not hypotheticals)
     - What you're specifically looking for (hypotheses to test)
   - **Next 10 min:** Peer review
     - Pair up and trade test plans
     - Partner identifies: Any leading questions? Tasks that give hints? Any hypothetical questions that should be grounded in past behavior?
     - Revise based on feedback

6. **In-Class Testing Session** (100 min) `[EQ3, U3, K3, K4, S3, S4]`
   - **Structure:** Rotate through 3 rounds of testing (~30 min each)
     - Round 1: You test a classmate's prototype (you are facilitator)
     - Round 2: A classmate tests your prototype (you observe)
     - Round 3: Switch partners, repeat
   - **During each test:**
     - Facilitator runs the 5 Act Method
     - Prototype owner observes silently (no helping!)
     - Both take notes
   - **Between rounds (5 min):** Quick debrief with your partner
   - **Final 10 min:** Individual reflection
     - What surprised you?
     - What usability issues emerged?
     - What did you learn about solution fit?
     - What will you change?
   - Instructor circulates to observe facilitation and offer feedback

**Total: 180 min**
