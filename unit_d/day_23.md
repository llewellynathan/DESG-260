# Day 23 — Monday, Mar 30
## Unit D: Neue App (Capstone)

**Focus:** A great product poorly communicated will not succeed — your case study is design work too.

**Why This Matters:** You've spent weeks researching, testing, and building. But none of that matters if you can't explain *why* you made the decisions you made. Employers don't hire portfolios full of pretty screens — they hire designers who can tell the story of their thinking. Today you learn how to turn your messy, iterative process into a compelling narrative that makes someone want to hire you.

**Targeted Learning Outcomes:**
- Detailed Documentation
- Communication and Feedback
- Design Strategy
- Critical Analysis of Form and Format

---

### Desired Results

**Essential Questions:**
- EQ1: What makes a case study compelling vs. just a collection of screenshots?
- EQ2: How do you tell the story of a design process that was messy and iterative?
- EQ3: How do you decide what to include — and what to leave out — in design documentation?

**Understandings:**
- U1: A case study is a narrative, not a report — the reader needs to understand your thinking, not just your output
- U2: The messy middle (iterations, failures, pivots) is more compelling than polished perfection — it shows how you think
- U3: Your case study is itself a design artifact — its typography, layout, and hierarchy should demonstrate the same skills as your app

**Students Will Know:**
- K1: Freytag's Pyramid as a case study framework: Exposition (who and why) → Rising Action (research and early prototypes) → Climax (the pivotal insight) → Falling Action (refinement) → Resolution (final product and reflection)
- K2: What each section of the D10 case study should contain and how it fits the narrative arc
- K3: Layout and typographic hierarchy principles for documentation design (title, section heads, body, captions)

**Students Will Be Able To:**
- S1: Articulate their project's story in a concise paragraph that makes sense to someone who's never seen their screens
- S2: Map their existing artifacts (research, wireframes, testing notes, screens) to case study sections
- S3: Begin assembling a case study with intentional narrative structure, not just chronological screenshots

### Activities

1. **Warm-Up: Testing Debrief Pairs** (10 min) `[EQ2, U2]`
   - Pair up with someone you haven't worked with recently
   - Share with your partner:
     - What was the #1 usability issue from your Round 2 testing?
     - What surprised you most about how people used your prototype?
     - What's the single most important change you need to make?
   - Whole-class share: 3–4 volunteers share their biggest surprise or most impactful planned change
   - Instructor frames: "These testing stories — the surprises, the failures, the pivots — are exactly what makes a great case study. Today we learn how to tell that story."

2. **Today's Learning Goals** (5 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, EQ3, U1, U2, U3]`
   - **Why this matters:** You've formed intent through research and validation. You've rendered intent through high-fidelity screens and animations. Now the final piece: *communicating* that intent. Your case study tells the story of your design process to people who weren't there.
   - A great product poorly documented is invisible. A well-told design story opens doors.
   - Preview: storytelling principles → case study structure → work session (iterate + begin documentation)
   - Connection to forming/rendering: The case study requires both — you form the narrative (what story to tell, what to emphasize) and render it (layout, hierarchy, visual quality of the document itself).

3. **Direct Instruction: Storytelling & the Case Study** (25 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, EQ3, U1, U2, U3, K1, K2, K3, S1]`

   **I do: Freytag's Pyramid — A Storytelling Framework** (5 min)
   - Your case study is not a lab report. It's a narrative.
   - The reader — an employer, a reviewer, a peer — wants to understand your *thinking*, not just your output.
   - Gustav Freytag studied what makes stories work and found a 5-stage arc. It maps perfectly to the design process:
     1. **Exposition:** Who has a problem? Why do *you* care? What's the inciting incident?
     2. **Rising Action:** What did you discover through research? What challenges emerged?
     3. **Climax:** What was the turning point — the test, insight, or decision that changed everything?
     4. **Falling Action:** How did you refine and iterate based on that insight?
     5. **Resolution:** What did you build? What were the outcomes? What did you learn?
   - Key question for every section: "What did I *learn*?" not "What did I *make*?"

   **I do: The Inciting Incident for School Projects** (3 min)
   - The inciting incident is the moment the problem became *personally real* to you.
   - For school projects, this is NOT "my professor assigned it." It's the moment you became genuinely invested.
   - Framings that work:
     - **Personal friction:** "I kept abandoning every habit tracker after a week because..."
     - **User interview surprise:** "During my Mom Test interviews, one person said something that reframed the whole problem..."
     - **Competitive gap:** "I tried every app in this space and none of them solved..."
     - **Observation:** "I watched my roommate spend 20 minutes doing something that should take 2..."
   - Be honest that it started as a class project — then show the moment it became real. That authenticity is more compelling than fabricating a client scenario.

   **I do: Mapping Your Case Study to the Pyramid** (7 min)
   - Walk through the D10 case study sections mapped to Freytag's five stages:
     - **Exposition:** Title page, creative brief, persona, your motivation → *Who is this for and why should we care?*
     - **Rising Action:** User research, competitive analysis, wireframes, paper prototypes → *What problems did you uncover? What was the landscape?*
     - **Climax:** The pivotal usability test or insight — before/after comparisons → *What changed your direction?*
     - **Falling Action:** Style guide, high-fidelity iteration, testing round 2, refinement → *How did you respond to what you learned?*
     - **Resolution:** Final screens, motion design, outcomes, reflection, AI usage documentation → *The polished solution + honest reflection*
   - The climax is the most important part — it's where you prove you can think, not just execute
   - Before/after comparisons (with annotations explaining *why* you changed it) are gold
   - "I tested it and discovered X, so I changed Y" is the most powerful sentence in a case study
   - Common mistakes:
     - Screenshot dump with no narrative thread
     - Skipping straight to the final screens (no story of how you got there)
     - Hiding the failures and iterations (the most interesting part!)
     - No climax — everything presented as a smooth, linear process (it never is)

   **We do: Story Thread Exercise** (5 min) `[S1]`
   - Write 5 sentences — one for each stage of the pyramid:
     1. **Exposition:** Who has the problem and why do *you* care?
     2. **Rising Action:** What did you discover through research and testing?
     3. **Climax:** What was the turning point — the insight or test that changed your direction?
     4. **Falling Action:** How did you refine your design in response?
     5. **Resolution:** What did you build and what did you learn?
   - Share with your partner. Does it make sense to someone who's never seen your screens? If not, revise.
   - This paragraph becomes the opening of your case study.

   **I do: Your Case Study Is a Design Artifact** (5 min) `[U3, K3]`
   - The document itself should demonstrate your design skills
   - Typographic hierarchy in documentation:
     - Title: large, bold — the project name and one-line description
     - Section heads: clear, consistent — guide the reader through the narrative
     - Body text: readable, well-spaced — the bulk of your story
     - Captions: small, paired with images — explain what the reader is seeing
   - Consistent page layout: margins, columns, image placement, whitespace
   - Use real content throughout — no "Lorem ipsum" or placeholder screenshots
   - Apply the same craft principles from Day 21 (hierarchy, spacing, restraint) to the document layout

4. **D10 Assignment Details** (5 min) `[K2, S2]`
   - **D10: Draft Documentation + Animated Prototype**
   - **Due: Wednesday, Apr 1 @ 11:55pm**
   - **Animated walkthrough video:**
     - Screen recording of your clickable prototype in action
     - Show: core user flow from onboarding to success state
     - Include at least 2 microinteractions/animations
     - Format: MP4 or MOV, 1–3 minutes
   - **Case study draft:**
     - All sections roughed in — this is a DRAFT, not the final
     - Focus on getting the **narrative structure** right: story arc, section flow, key artifacts placed
     - Polish comes later (Day 24+)
     - Each section should have: header, relevant artifacts (screenshots, diagrams, photos), and at least a few sentences of narrative text explaining what you did and what you learned
   - Submit: PDF + video to Learning Suite

5. **Work Session: Iterate + Begin Documentation** (75 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, EQ3, U1, U2, U3, K1, K2, K3, S1, S2, S3]`

   Three structured rounds. Not free time — each round has a specific goal.

   **Round 1: Apply Testing Feedback** (25 min)
   - Review your D9 testing notes. Identify your top 3 most critical issues.
   - Make the changes that matter most to your Figma screens.
   - For each change: take a "before" screenshot first. You'll need before/after comparisons for your case study.
   - Priority: fix usability issues > add polish > add new screens
   - Instructor circulates with feedback on design iteration

   **Round 2: Narrative Outline** (25 min)
   - Open a new page in Figma (or a Google Doc) for your case study layout
   - Write a one-line summary for each section: what artifact goes here? What's the story?
   - Map your existing work to sections:
     - D1 → Title page, creative brief
     - D2 → User research, persona, journey map
     - D3 → Competitive analysis
     - D5 → Wireframes, paper prototypes
     - D6/D9 → Usability testing documentation (both rounds)
     - D7 → Style guide, branding
     - D8 → High-fidelity screens
     - AI tools used throughout → AI usage documentation
   - Identify gaps: which sections need new writing? Which just need screenshots + captions?

   **Round 3: Begin Assembly** (25 min)
   - Start building your case study document
   - Set up your page layout: margins, columns, type hierarchy
   - Begin with Act 1 (setup): title page, creative brief, persona
   - Place artifacts and write section intros
   - Don't aim for perfection — aim for structure. Get the bones in place.
   - Instructor circulates with feedback on documentation structure and narrative flow

**Total: 120 min**
