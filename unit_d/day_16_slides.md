### Slide 8: Synthesis Framework
**Look for patterns, not quotes**
- **Frequency:** How many people mentioned it?
- **Intensity:** How much did it bother them?
- **Behavior vs. opinion:** What people *do* matters more than what they *say* they'd do

**Red flag vs. green flag:**
- "I would totally use that!" — hypothetical, weak signal
- "I spent 2 hours last week trying to solve this" — real pain, strong signal

### Slide 12: Decision Criteria
**How to know if your idea is validated**
- **Frequency:** Did multiple people describe this problem?
- **Intensity:** How much does it actually bother them?
- **Current solutions:** Are they already trying to solve it? (Good sign — proves the need)
- **Willingness to act:** Have they spent time/money/effort?

The goal is NOT to find a problem nobody has — it's to find a problem people actually care about solving.

### Slide 15: Why Competitors Matter
**If your idea is truly novel, that's often a warning sign.**

Maybe nobody needs it.

Good ideas exist in a space with competitors — your job is to understand them and differentiate.

You're not copying. You're learning from what exists.

---

### Slide 16: Components of Competitive Analysis
**What D3 requires:**
- **4 competitors** (any mix of digital/physical products)
- **Positioning matrix:** 2x2 chart with differentiating dimensions
  - Example axes: Simple ↔ Complex, Social ↔ Individual
  - Plot competitors and identify whitespace
- **2-paragraph summary:** Who's in the space and what they're doing

---

### Slide 17: AI for Competitive Research
**AI can help:**
- Summarize app store reviews
- Identify competitors you missed
- Analyze feature sets

**AI limitations:**
- Doesn't know what users actually experience
- You must download and use the apps yourself

**Critical:** AI supplements hands-on research. Never replaces it.
