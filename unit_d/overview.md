# Unit D: Neue App
## Problem Seeking, Product as Beautiful and Functional Interface

This is it — the capstone. Everything you've learned about forming and rendering intent comes together in a single project: a brand-new product you'll design from scratch.

### The Big Idea

In previous units, you practiced pieces of the design process in isolation. Unit A focused on rendering (crafting a visual system). Unit B balanced both but started with an existing product. Unit C emphasized hierarchy and responsiveness. Now you face the full challenge: **discovering a problem worth solving AND executing a solution with professional-quality craft.**

This is what professional product design looks like. No one hands you a brief. You identify the opportunity through research and observation. You validate your assumptions by talking to real people. You develop a visual identity that serves both usability and brand. And you document the whole journey so others can understand — and be convinced by — your work.

**The best design opportunities come from curiosity and observation, not from briefs.** Forming intent means discovering what's actually worth building. Rendering intent means building it beautifully. Excellence in both is non-negotiable.

### What You'll Learn

- **Problem seeking over problem solving.** You'll practice generating, validating, and scoping product ideas before committing to execution. The hardest part of design isn't building — it's knowing what to build.

- **Research as a design activity.** Interviews, competitive analysis, and observational research aren't preliminary steps — they're how designers form intent. You'll conduct real user research and synthesize findings into personas, journey maps, and user flows.

- **Visual identity serves usability.** Brand, typography, color, and motion aren't decorative — they communicate meaning, establish trust, and guide behavior. You'll develop a cohesive visual system that reinforces your product's purpose.

- **Prototyping at multiple fidelities.** From paper sketches to interactive Figma prototypes with motion, you'll work at the right level of detail for each stage of the process.

- **Documentation as storytelling.** A great product poorly communicated will not succeed. You'll learn to present your work in a portfolio-ready case study format.

### What You'll Make

A brand-new digital product designed for at least two platforms (mobile phone primary, plus tablet/watch/desktop/glasses/car/etc.). Your final deliverables include:

1. **An animated walkthrough video** demonstrating core interactions and microanimations
2. **A comprehensive case study PDF** documenting your entire process — from initial research through high-fidelity screens

The case study tells the story of your design decisions: why this problem, why this audience, why this solution.

### Why This Matters

This project is your first portfolio piece that demonstrates the full cycle of product design. Employers and graduate programs want to see that you can identify opportunities, conduct research, make strategic decisions, execute with craft, and communicate your process clearly. The Neue App project proves you can do all of it.

The habits you build here — rigorous research, iterative testing, cohesive visual systems, clear documentation — are exactly what distinguish junior designers who get hired from those who don't.
