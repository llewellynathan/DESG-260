## Why Competitive Analysis Matters

Before you design anything, you need to understand who else is solving your problem. Competitive analysis helps you:

- **Avoid reinventing the wheel** — Learn from what competitors do well
- **Find your differentiation** — Identify gaps and opportunities they've missed
- **Ground your design decisions** — "We do X because competitors do Y and users complained about it"
- **Validate market demand** — If no one is solving this problem, ask why

**Important:** Having competitors is a *good* sign. It proves people care about this problem. Your job is to understand them and do something meaningfully different.

## The Scaffolded Approach

Don't start with the positioning matrix. Start with experience, then let the insights build toward positioning.

### Step 1: Experience the Competitors (Hands-On)

**Do this first.** Download and actually use 2-4 competitor apps. Spend at least 15-20 minutes with each.

**While using each app, note:**
- First impressions — What's the onboarding like? How do you feel?
- Core flow — How easy is it to accomplish the main task?
- Friction points — Where do you get stuck or frustrated?
- Delights — What's surprisingly good or clever?
- Visual/brand impression — Does it feel premium? Playful? Clinical?

**Capture:**
- Screenshots of key screens
- Voice memos or written notes as you use it
- Your honest reaction as a user, not as a designer critiquing

**Why this matters:** You cannot analyze what you haven't experienced. App store descriptions and feature lists don't tell you what it *feels like* to use something.

---

### Step 2: Mine User Reviews

Read what real users say about your competitors. This reveals what people actually care about — not what the company *thinks* they care about.

**For each competitor:**
- Read 10-15 app store reviews (mix of positive and negative)
- Look for patterns: What do multiple people praise? Complain about?
- Note specific language users use — this is how your audience talks

**What to extract:**
- **Common praise:** "I love how simple it is," "The reminders actually work"
- **Common complaints:** "Too many features," "Crashes constantly," "Subscription is ridiculous"
- **Unmet needs:** "I wish it had..." or "If only it could..."

**Using AI for review mining:**
AI can help you process more reviews faster. Try prompts like:
- "Summarize the top 5 complaints from these 20 reviews"
- "What features do users praise most often?"
- "What unmet needs do users express?"

⚠️ **Critical:** AI summarizes, but you must verify. Read at least 10 reviews yourself per competitor. AI can miss nuance, sentiment, and context.

---

### Step 3: Strengths & Weaknesses Summary

For each competitor, synthesize what you learned into a clear summary.

**Template per competitor:**

```
## [Competitor Name]
**What it is:** [One sentence description]

**Strengths (3-4 bullets):**
- [What they do well, grounded in your experience and reviews]

**Weaknesses (3-4 bullets):**
- [What they do poorly or what users complain about]

**Key insight:** [One sentence about what you learned from this competitor]
```

**Example:**

```
## Headspace
**What it is:** Guided meditation app with courses and daily content

**Strengths:**
- Beautiful, calming visual design that reinforces the brand
- Structured courses make it easy for beginners to start
- High production quality on audio content
- Strong brand recognition and trust

**Weaknesses:**
- Expensive subscription ($70/year) prices out casual users
- Limited customization — you follow their path, not yours
- Daily reminders feel naggy according to reviews
- Content library can feel overwhelming

**Key insight:** Headspace trades flexibility for structure — great for beginners, frustrating for experienced meditators who want control.
```

### Step 4: Positioning Matrix

Now — and only now — create your 2x2 positioning matrix. The axes should emerge naturally from what you learned in Steps 1-3.

**How to find your axes:**

Look at your strengths/weaknesses summaries. What trade-offs did you notice?

- Did some apps feel "simple but limited" while others felt "powerful but overwhelming"? → **Simple ↔ Feature-rich**
- Did some focus on "building habits" while others were "one-time tools"? → **Habit-focused ↔ Goal-focused**
- Did some feel "social and community-driven" while others were "private and individual"? → **Social ↔ Individual**
- Did some "guide you through everything" while others "let you figure it out"? → **Guided ↔ Self-directed**

**Test your axes:**
1. Do your competitors spread across the quadrants? (If they all cluster, pick different axes)
2. Would your interviewees describe this as a meaningful trade-off?
3. Does the whitespace represent a real opportunity, or just a bad idea?

**Create the matrix:**
- Draw a 2x2 grid with your two axes
- Plot your 4 competitors
- Identify where YOUR app would sit
- The whitespace is your opportunity — but only if users actually want what's there

## Using AI Throughout

AI can accelerate competitive analysis, but it cannot replace hands-on research.

### Where AI Helps

| Task | How AI helps | What you still must do |
|------|--------------|------------------------|
| Finding competitors | "What apps compete with [your idea]?" | Verify they exist; download and use them |
| Summarizing reviews | Process 50+ reviews quickly | Read 10-15 yourself per competitor |
| Identifying patterns | "What themes appear in these complaints?" | Validate patterns match your experience |
| Drafting summaries | Generate initial strengths/weaknesses | Edit based on your actual usage |
| Suggesting axes | "What dimensions differentiate these apps?" | Test axes against your research |

### Where AI Fails

- **AI doesn't know what it feels like to use an app.** It can read descriptions, not experiences.
- **AI can hallucinate features or competitors.** Always verify.
- **AI reflects existing content, not your unique interviews.** Your user research is primary.
- **AI can miss nuance.** "Users say it's 'simple'" — is that praise or complaint? Context matters.

### Prompts That Work

```
"I'm analyzing competitors for a [type of app]. Based on these app store
reviews [paste reviews], what are the top 3 things users praise and the
top 3 things they complain about?"

"Here are my notes on 4 competitors [paste notes]. What trade-offs or
dimensions seem to differentiate them? Suggest 2-3 possible axes for
a positioning matrix."

"I'm considering these axes for my positioning matrix: [Axis A] vs [Axis B].
Based on what you know about [competitor names], does this create meaningful
differentiation, or would they all cluster together?"
```

## How to Know You Did It Right

✓ You actually downloaded and used at least 2-3 competitor apps
✓ You read real user reviews, not just app store descriptions
✓ Your strengths/weaknesses are specific, not generic ("good UI" is vague; "calming color palette reinforces meditation brand" is specific)
✓ Your axes emerged from your research, not from guessing
✓ Competitors spread across your matrix (not clustered in one quadrant)
✓ You can explain where YOUR app sits and why that's an opportunity
✓ You documented how you used AI (or didn't)

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **Analyzing apps you've never used.** Screenshots and descriptions aren't enough.
❌ **Choosing axes before doing research.** Let the axes emerge from what you learn.
❌ **Generic strengths/weaknesses.** "Good design" means nothing. Be specific.
❌ **Ignoring negative reviews.** Complaints reveal opportunities.
❌ **Trusting AI summaries without verification.** AI hallucinates. Check everything.
❌ **Choosing axes where everyone clusters.** If all competitors are in one quadrant, your axes aren't differentiating.
❌ **Whitespace that nobody wants.** Just because a quadrant is empty doesn't mean it's an opportunity.
