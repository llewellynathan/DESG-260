### Slide 6: Why This Matters
**Why this matters**
- Every platform has different strengths, constraints, and user expectations.
- A watch rewards glanceable info. A tablet rewards rich layouts. A desktop rewards density and keyboard shortcuts.
- Adapting your app isn't resizing — it's rethinking what the platform is *good at* and redesigning around that.

### Slide 8: Three Dimensions That Change
**What changes across platforms**
- **Screen real estate:** Phone (moderate) → Watch (tiny) → Tablet (expansive) → Desktop (wide)
- **Input modality:** Touch → Crown + small touch → Keyboard + mouse → Voice + gaze
- **Context of use:** Where, when, and how long people use each device

---

### Slide 9: What Stays the Same
**What stays the same**

- **Mental model** — the user's understanding of what the app does

Same forming, different rendering.

### Slide 10: Platform Conventions
**Breaking conventions costs trust**
- Users bring expectations from every other app on that platform
- Navigation patterns differ: tab bar (phone), side menu (tablet), menu bar (desktop), complications (watch)
- Information density differs: watch shows 1-2 data points; desktop shows tables, sidebars, dashboards

---

### Slide 11: Quick Platform Reference
**Platform cheat sheet**
- **Watch:** Glanceable. 1 action per screen. Sessions < 15 sec. Digital Crown scrolling.
- **Tablet:** More room ≠ bigger phone. Split views, sidebars, popovers. Multi-column. Pencil input.
- **Desktop:** Keyboard shortcuts, hover states, right-click. Dense info display. Persistent nav.
- **Car:** Minimal interaction. Voice-first. Large targets. No reading.

> What's one convention on your chosen platform that your phone app doesn't have?

---

### Slide 12: The Translation Framework
**Deciding what survives**
- **Carry** — Core screens that work on both platforms (with layout changes)
- **Simplify** — Screens that need to be stripped down for the new platform
- **Expand** — Screens that benefit from more space or new input methods
- **Remove** — Screens that don't make sense on the new platform
- **Add** — New screens that leverage the platform's unique strengths

---

### Slide 13: Translation Example
**Example: Budgeting app → Watch**
- **Carry:** Transaction logging (simplified to amount + category)
- **Simplify:** Dashboard → today's spending total only
- **Remove:** Detailed reports, category management, goal setting
- **Add:** Complication showing remaining daily budget, haptic alerts for overspending

> Run through your app's screens mentally — which category does each fall into?
