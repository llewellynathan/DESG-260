# Day 22 — Wednesday, Mar 25
## Unit D: Neue App (Capstone)

**Focus:** Testing is the fastest way to find what your eyes can't see.

**Why This Matters:** You've been staring at your own screens for days. You know where everything is, so everything "makes sense" to you. But your users don't have that context. A 10-minute test session will reveal more about what needs to change than another hour of self-critique. Today is about getting your high-fidelity work in front of real eyes — classmates first, then outside users for D9.

**Targeted Learning Outcomes:**
- Better Interactions & Experiences
- Critical Analysis of Form and Format
- Communication and Feedback
- Design Strategy

---

### Desired Results

**Essential Questions:**
- EQ1: What can testing reveal that self-review cannot?
- EQ2: How does testing change when the prototype looks "real" instead of rough?

**Understandings:**
- U1: High-fidelity prototypes change how users behave in tests — they focus on details (color, copy, spacing) rather than concepts, so your testing focus must shift too
- U2: Motion design serves usability — it communicates state changes, confirms actions, and guides attention

**Students Will Know:**
- K1: How to set up a clickable Figma prototype for testing (hotspots, flows, starting frame)
- K2: What to observe during high-fidelity testing vs. earlier rounds (polish issues, hesitations, misreads)
- K3: The purpose of microinteractions: confirm, guide, connect, brand

**Students Will Be Able To:**
- S1: Facilitate a usability test of their high-fidelity prototype and document actionable findings
- S2: Identify at least 3 specific issues from peer testing to address before D9
- S3: Set up Smart Animate transitions between key screens in Figma

### Activities

1. **Warm-Up: D8 Desk Crit** (10 min) `[EQ1, U1, K2]`
   - Open your D8 screens on your laptop
   - Turn to a neighbor and spend 3 minutes each: show your screens, get first impressions
   - Prompt: "What's the first thing your eye goes to? Does that match what I want you to see first?"
   - This is a quick gut-check, not a full critique — save detailed feedback for testing

2. **Today's Learning Goals** (5 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, U1, U2]`
   - **Why this matters:** You've done the forming — validated the problem, mapped the flow, built the system, started rendering high-fidelity screens. Today is about closing the loop: put your rendered work in front of users to discover what still needs forming.
   - Preview:
     - Quick overview: how high-fi testing differs from earlier rounds
     - In-class peer testing sessions
     - Brief motion design intro, then extended work time
   - Most of today is yours to work — use it well

3. **Direct Instruction: Testing High-Fi + Motion Design Essentials** (15 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, U1, U2, K1, K2, K3]`

   **High-Fi Testing (8 min):**
   - **I do:** Show how to set up a clickable Figma prototype
     - Set a starting frame, add hotspot connections between screens
     - Present mode: Share → "Anyone with the link" for remote testing
     - Tip: Hide Figma UI — present full-screen so it feels like a real app
   - **I do:** What changes when the prototype looks real
     - Users stop saying "I get the idea" and start noticing details
     - Watch for: hesitations, misreads of hierarchy, tapping the wrong thing, confused expressions
     - Your testing questions shift from "Does this concept make sense?" to "Can you complete this task without help?"
   - **We do:** Quick show of hands — who has at least 5 screens connected? Who needs to set up hotspots today?

   **Motion Design in 5 Minutes (7 min):**
   - **I do:** Why motion matters — it's not decoration, it's communication
     - 4 jobs of microinteractions: **Confirm** (button press), **Guide** (drawer slides in), **Connect** (shared element transitions), **Brand** (playful loading state)
     - Smart Animate in Figma: match layer names between frames → Figma interpolates the difference
     - Demo: one simple Smart Animate transition (e.g., card expanding to detail view)
   - You watched the tutorial — today's work session is your chance to try it
   - D9 requires at least 2 custom microinteractions, so start experimenting today

4. **In-Class Peer Testing** (40 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, U1, K1, K2, S1, S2]`
   - Pair up with someone you haven't worked with recently
   - **Round 1 (15 min):** Person A tests Person B's prototype
     - Tester: Give 2-3 realistic tasks ("You want to log today's workout — show me how")
     - Observer: Watch silently. Note where they hesitate, tap wrong, or ask questions.
     - Debrief together: Top 3 observations
   - **Round 2 (15 min):** Switch roles
   - **Wrap-up (10 min):** Individually, write down:
     - 3 specific issues found
     - For each: what you'll change and why
     - This becomes the start of your D9 documentation

5. **Work Session: Build, Animate, Polish** (110 min) `[EQ2, U1, U2, K1, K2, K3, S1, S2, S3]`
   - **Priority 1:** Fix issues from peer testing — address the biggest problems first
   - **Priority 2:** Continue building high-fidelity screens toward your 5-screen minimum
   - **Priority 3:** Experiment with Smart Animate — try at least one transition
   - **Priority 4:** Set up your prototype for outside testing (D9 requires 3+ people outside class)
   - Instructor circulates for 1:1 feedback
   - Use the 6-principle checklist from Day 21 as you work:
     1. Hierarchy: 3 levels visible in 3 seconds?
     2. Spacing: 8pt grid, groups clearly separated?
     3. Typography: 4 sizes max, correct letter spacing?
     4. Color: accent at ~10%, all text passes 4.5:1?
     5. Alignment: left edges align, concentric radii?
     6. Restraint: can you remove anything without losing info?

**Total: 180 min**
