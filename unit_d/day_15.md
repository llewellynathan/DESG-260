# Day 15 — Monday, Mar 2
## Unit D: Neue App (Capstone)

**Focus:** Treating your ideas as hypotheses — "What if I'm wrong?"

**Why This Matters:** Every designer has had a "great idea" that users didn't actually want. The difference between amateur and professional product design is validation — learning to test your assumptions before investing weeks of work. Today you'll learn the Mom Test framework, which teaches you how to have honest conversations with potential users. This skill will save you from building products nobody needs — in this class and in your career.

**Targeted Learning Outcomes:**
- Problem Discovery & Research (Curiosity, Empathy, Humility)
- Design Strategy
- Communication and Feedback

---

### Desired Results

**Essential Questions:**
- EQ1: How do you know if your idea solves a real problem?
- EQ2: Why is it dangerous to fall in love with your first idea?
- EQ3: How do you ask questions that reveal truth instead of confirming what you want to hear?

**Understandings:**
- U1: Ideas are hypotheses to test, not solutions to build
- U2: User research reveals truth only when you ask the right questions the right way
- U3: Validation isn't about proving yourself right — it's about learning what's actually true

**Students Will Know:**
- K1: The Mom Test principles (don't ask leading questions, ask about past behavior, don't pitch your idea)
- K2: The difference between validation and confirmation bias
- K3: How to structure and conduct a validation interview
- K4: What makes a strong vs. weak opportunity statement or job story

**Students Will Be Able To:**
- S1: Evaluate the strength of their D1 hypotheses through peer discussion
- S2: Write non-leading interview questions using the Mom Test framework
- S3: Plan and prepare for validation interviews with 4+ potential users

### Activities

1. **Warm-Up: Share with Your Neighbors** (10 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, K4, S1]`
   - Turn to 2-3 people near you and take turns sharing your favorite opportunity statement or job story
   - As you listen, consider: Does this sound like a real problem or an invented solution?
   - Quick reactions only — save deeper feedback for Activity 4
   - Goal: Get your ideas out of your head and hear how they land with others

2. **Today's Learning Goals** (5 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, EQ3, U1, U2, U3]`
   - **Why this matters:** You have ideas — but you don't know yet if they solve real problems. Today you'll learn how to find out.
   - Preview:
     - Direct instruction: The risk of unvalidated ideas + the Mom Test framework
     - Peer discussion: Strengthen your hypotheses with classmate feedback
     - Work session: Prepare your interview questions and plan who to talk to
   - Connection to forming/rendering: We are deep in *forming intent* — discovering the right problem before we render anything

3. **Direct Instruction: Risk Minimization & The Mom Test** (25 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, EQ3, U1, U2, U3, K1, K2, K3, S2]`

   **Why validation matters** (5 min)
   - **I do:** Present the risk minimization mindset
     - Most startups fail because they build something nobody wants — not because the product was ugly
     - "What if I'm wrong?" is the most valuable question a designer can ask
     - Your D1 submissions are *hypotheses*, not commitments — treat them that way
     - The goal of the next week: learn enough to commit to ONE direction with confidence

   **The Mom Test** (15 min)
   - **I do:** Introduce the core principles
     - Named after the idea that even your mom will lie to you about your idea (she loves you too much to be honest)
     - **Rule 1: Talk about their life, not your idea.** Don't pitch — ask about their current behavior.
     - **Rule 2: Ask about the past, not the future.** "Tell me about the last time you tried to..." not "Would you use an app that..."
     - **Rule 3: Talk less, listen more.** If you're talking more than 30% of the time, you're doing it wrong.
     - **Rule 4: Seek disconfirming evidence.** Actively look for reasons your idea might be wrong.
   - Bad question: "Would you use an app that helps you track your water intake?" (Leading, hypothetical)
   - Good question: "Tell me about the last time you felt dehydrated. What happened?" (Past behavior, open-ended)
   - Bad question: "Don't you think budgeting apps are confusing?" (Leading)
   - Good question: "Walk me through how you managed your money last month." (Past behavior, neutral)
   - **We do:** Transform bad questions together
     - Give 2-3 bad questions; class rewrites them using Mom Test principles
     - Example: "Would you pay for a meditation app?" → "What did you try the last time you felt stressed? What happened?"

   **Structuring a validation interview** (5 min)
   - **I do:** Walk through a 15-minute interview structure:
     1. Context (2 min): "Tell me about yourself and your relationship with [topic area]"
     2. Current behavior (5 min): "Walk me through the last time you [relevant activity]"
     3. Pain points (5 min): "What was frustrating about that? What did you wish was different?"
     4. Existing solutions (3 min): "Have you tried anything to solve this? What happened?"
   - Don't mention your app idea during the interview — you're gathering data, not selling

4. **D1 Peer Discussion & Critique** (20 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, K4, S1]`
   - Groups of 3-4 students
   - Each person shares their top hypothesis (2 min each)
   - Group discusses:
     - Does this sound like a real problem or an invented solution?
     - Who actually has this problem? Can you name specific people?
     - What assumptions are embedded in this hypothesis? How would you test them?
     - Which of your 3 ideas has the strongest signal from real life?
   - After discussion: each student identifies their strongest hypothesis to validate first
   - Reminder: You're NOT committing to an app yet — that comes after validation (D2)

5. **Work Session: D2 Interview Preparation + 1:1 Feedback** (60 min) `[EQ3, U2, U3, K1, K3, S2, S3]`
   - **First 15 min:** Write your interview questions
     - Draft 8-10 questions using Mom Test principles
     - Include context, current behavior, pain points, and existing solutions
     - Self-check: Cross out any question that mentions your app idea or leads the witness
   - **Next 15 min:** Practice interviews
     - Pair up and practice interviewing each other
     - Partner gives feedback: "That question felt leading because..." or "I wanted to tell you more about..."
   - **Remaining 30 min:** Plan your research
     - Identify 4+ people to interview before Day 16 (Wednesday)
     - These should be potential users, NOT friends who will just agree with you
     - Schedule interviews (in person, phone, or video)
     - Begin D2 documentation template
   - Instructor circulates for 1:1 feedback on hypotheses and interview questions

**Total: 120 min**
