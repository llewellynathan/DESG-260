For this project you’ll build upon the processes you learned in project one to create your own brand-new interactive product. 

## Product strategy

Any new digital product succeeds only when it can convince people to use it instead of something else. Your research will need to demonstrate how your product will better serve the intended audience.

That does not mean every part of your digital product will be completely new. For most of you, your product will build upon previous competitors—you will be improving the interface, tying it more closely to a brand or shifting the brand into new directions, and in every case making it easier to use. At the same time, you need to recognize what works: don’t fix what isn’t broken. 

## Ideas

Your app should involve some sort of personal improvement; and could fall under one of these categories: Education/ (Entertainment/News — if focused on personal improvement), Productivity, Health/Fitness, or Finance. Within these broad categories, you will narrow your audience and design for specific use cases. 

The primary function should not be a social media platform or a shopping platform (although your app can have limited social sharing or commercial features). 

**Your app cannot be: a dating app, product/e-commerce, or social media app**

Your app must be designed for at least two platforms. These can include:

- Tablet
- Watch
- Desktop
- Glasses/Vision Pro
- Car Dashboard
- Or any other peripheral device (fitness band, thermostat, speaker, car, etc.). 
    

NOTE: *For most of you, your primary interactive product will take the form of a mobile phone app. However, if your research insights reveal a better form (a website, an Apple Watch app, or other interactive screen), **you may design for a different platform with permission.***

## Scope

Because this is a completely new interactive product, you’ll be expected to design the core function of the app from on-boarding to success screen* as well as the look and feel of the product from the brand to logos and icons.

You will need to demonstrate how your design improves usability through research, testing, and careful documentation. Work through the entire functionality of the app and design accordingly. 

### Bespoke design and existing patterns

Your design needs to show an appropriate balance between custom, brand-specific elements and clever use of existing patterns. Since you are creating from the ground up, you should generally adhere to Apple’s Human Interface Guidelines or Google’s Material Design Guidelines. Deviate from established UX patterns deliberately and with good reason. 

### Branding, typography, and motion

Your product should include a custom designed app logo and word mark (although the focus of this project is not branding).  All products need to show appropriate typographic hierarchy and carefully chosen custom typography. No default typography.

All projects need to show at least 2 animations of new micro-interactions.     

*NOTE: the main function of your app should be understandable within 3 or 4 screens (not counting on-boarding/signing in). On-boarding and additional features may increase the number of screens you need, but seek guidance on how to pare down the interactive product if you have dramatically more than this.*

## AI Usage Policy

AI tools (V0, Lovable, Bolt.new, Figma Make, etc.) are powerful **validation tools**. Use them to quickly test whether your concept works — generate a coded prototype, put it in front of users, and learn fast.

However, your AI prototype is a **disposable artifact**, not your final design. All high-fidelity screens must be **hand-built in Figma from scratch**. You may reference the AI output for layout ideas, but your final design should reflect your own intentional decisions about spacing, hierarchy, typography, color, and interaction patterns.

**Why?** The AI can render a plausible interface, but it can't form intent. Your job is to demonstrate that you can do both — discover the right problem (forming) AND craft a beautiful, functional solution (rendering). If the AI does the rendering for you, you've only done half the work.

- How they currently are solving (or not solving) the problem you’re tackling

[ ] Decide on your market demographic

THIS IS HUGE. Like we’ve talked about in class, we can’t effectively design if we don’t know who we’re designing for. When we don’t know our audience, it’s like trying to sew an outfit for someone—if we don’t know who we’re sewing for, the chances of it fitting and making them look good are very, very low.
