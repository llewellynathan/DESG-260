### Slide 5: Platform Conventions
**Platform Conventions & When to Break Them** (25 min)

---

### Slide 6: Why Conventions Exist
- **Reduce cognitive load** — Users don't have to think about how the interface works
- **Enable transfer learning** — Skills from one app carry over to another
- **Build trust** — Predictable behavior makes users feel confident and in control
- **Speed up task completion** — Familiar patterns let users act without hesitation

---

### Slide 7: iOS vs. Android — Key Differences
- **Navigation:** iOS uses edge swipe + back buttons in nav bar; Android uses system back button
- **Tab bars:** iOS bottom tabs; Android often uses bottom nav OR top tabs
- **Typography:** San Francisco (iOS) vs. Roboto (Android)
- **Actions:** iOS often uses action sheets; Android uses bottom sheets

---

### Slide 8: iOS vs. Android
Native apps *feel* native when they follow their platform's conventions.

### Slide 9: When Is Breaking Conventions Justified?
- **Good break:** Snapchat's camera-first launch — broke convention, but served core purpose
- **Bad break:** Non-standard confirmation dialogs that confuse users
- **Rule of thumb:** Break conventions only when the benefit clearly outweighs the learning cost

### Slide 11: Task Flows vs. User Flows
**Task Flows vs. User Flows** (10 min)

---

### Slide 12: The Pancake Analogy
**Task flow** = the ideal linear path, no branches
- "Making a pancake": preheat → gather → mix → cook → flip → eat
- Shows the *system's perspective* — what the design expects users to do

**User flow** = the real experience with decisions and branches
- "Mimi, a parent of three, making pancakes": START → Do I have all ingredients? → Can I substitute? → [task flow] → Make more? → END
- Shows a *specific persona's perspective* — including decision points, errors, alternate paths

---

### Slide 13: Key Insight
Task flows can be *embedded within* larger user flows.

For B2, you're creating two task diagrams — the existing app flow and a modified flow with your new feature added. Use color or a visual separator to demarcate the new feature from the original.

### Slide 15: Paper Prototyping Technique
- One screen per card or sheet of paper
- Draw enough detail that someone can "tap" elements while you flip pages
- Focus on structure and flow, not polish — paper is for thinking, not impressing
