# Assignment 2: Make It Better App

## "One More Thing"

"One more thing" became a catchphrase of Steve Jobs during his famous Apple launches. After showcasing a wonderful product, he'd surprise the audience by saving the best feature for last. And yet, even with all of the amazing digital software products we use every day, isn't there always something missing?


## ✏️ The Assignment

How many times have you started using an app and thought "I could make this better"? That's what we'll be doing. For this project, you will **add a feature or user interaction** to one of the default Apple iOS or Google Android applications (Contacts, Phone, Reminders, Messages, Stocks, Weather, Mail, etc.).

You will research, design, and prototype the new interaction such that your additions will be **consistent with the original application's design language**, pass usability tests, and still accomplish the core function of the app for the latest phone and operating system possible.

This project will introduce you to processes and concepts to improve your user experience skills and critical thinking.


### CHOOSING YOUR APP

💚 Pick a default Apple iOS or Google Android app (Contacts, Phone, Reminders, Messages, Stocks, Weather, Mail, etc.)
💚 The app should have a clear opportunity for a new feature or improved interaction
💚 Focus on one primary feature addition, not a full redesign
❌ Don't pick third-party apps
❌ Don't pick apps that require specialized domain knowledge you don't have


## 🚃 Process

### Deliverable

A well-designed **documentation PDF** that includes the components below. Most of these will be completed as small exercises first and then integrated into the full project documentation. The documentation should demonstrate a cohesive format with clear typographic hierarchy and carefully chosen grids, typefaces, and layout — illuminating the app design, not clashing with nor overshadowing it.

**The PDF should include:**

1. **Title Page with Creative Brief** — Goal of the new interaction
2. **User Flow of Existing App** — With critique (pros/cons)
3. **Competitive Analysis**
    - Top-level competitive analysis (current iOS/Android app + 3-4 other apps + 1 physical product)
    - Quadrant positioning chart
4. **User Task Diagrams**
    - Complete user flow of existing app
    - Modified user flow with your new feature added
    - These can be on the same page or two separate pages; make sure the new feature is clearly demarcated from the original using color or a similar visual separator
5. **Paper Prototype + Iterative Prototypes** (either digital wireframes or other iterative designs)
    - Show feedback from user tests and notes about behind-the-scenes features
    - Include the first prototype; make clear where your edits came from — demonstrate your iterative design process
    - Note your ideas and reasoning for creative choices
    - No need to include device mock-ups at this stage
6. **High-Fidelity Final Prototype**
    - Show the finished design in all of its glory (include device mock-up)
    - Should be indistinguishable from Apple or Google default apps
    - Include annotations about features or careful design decisions


## B1 User Flow & Competitive Analysis

Before you can improve something, you need to understand it.

**User Flow:** Map the current experience of your chosen default app. Document every screen, every tap, every decision point in the flow you're adding to. Note the app's strengths and weaknesses (pros/cons).

**Competitive Analysis Matrix:** Analyze 3-4 apps that address a similar need: your chosen default app + 3-4 other digital apps + 1 physical product (if you can find one). Create a quadrant positioning chart (a matrix).

Turn in a PDF to Learning Suite (due Day 6, end of class)


## B2a Competitive Analysis Matrix

Turn in a PDF to Learning Suite (due Day 6, end of class)


## B2 Paper Prototype + User Task Diagram

Sketch your new feature on paper. Don't open Figma yet — paper forces you to focus on structure, not pixels.

**Paper Prototype:** Draw your screens showing the new feature integrated into the existing app. Include enough detail that someone could "use" the prototype by pointing at buttons while you flip pages.

**User Task Diagram:** Create two diagrams — the existing user flow and the modified flow with your new feature added. Use color or a visual separator to demarcate the new feature from the original.

Test your paper prototype with at least 1 person (not a classmate). Note what confused them.

Turn in to Learning Suite (due before Day 7)


## B3 Digital Prototype + User Testing Round 2

Translate your paper prototype into a digital prototype and test it with real users.

**Digital Prototype:** Build your new feature as an interactive Figma prototype. Match the existing app's design language (typography, spacing, color, interaction patterns). Include enough screens to demonstrate the complete new feature flow.

**User Testing:** Test your digital prototype with at least 2 people (not classmates). Document what worked, what confused users, and what you plan to change.

**Submit:** A PDF with a link to your Figma prototype and notes from user testing. Format them together into a slide for your final project documentation. See Learning Suite content section about this project for best documentation practices.

Turn in to Learning Suite (due before Day 8)
