# Assignment 2: Make It Better App

## "One More Thing"

Steve Jobs famously used "one more thing" to introduce unexpected product announcements. But in product design, the real magic isn't adding features — it's fixing what's broken. The best redesigns don't announce themselves; users just stop being frustrated.


## ✏️ The Assignment

Choose an existing mobile app that frustrates you (or someone you know). Analyze what's wrong, test your assumptions with real users, and redesign the core experience. Your goal isn't to reimagine the entire app — it's to identify one significant problem and solve it convincingly.


### CHOOSING YOUR APP

💚 Pick an app you actually use (or used to use before giving up on it)
💚 The app should have a clear purpose that it's failing to achieve well
💚 Focus on one primary user flow or feature, not the whole app
❌ Don't pick apps that are already well-designed (Instagram, Spotify, etc.)
❌ Don't pick apps that require specialized domain knowledge you don't have


### REDESIGN REQUIREMENTS

💚 Your redesign must follow platform conventions (iOS or Android) unless you can justify the deviation
💚 Include at least 5 screens showing the complete user flow
💚 Document at least 2 usability tests with real users (not classmates)
💚 Show before/after comparisons for key screens
❌ Don't redesign the visual brand (logo, colors, typography) — focus on UX

## 🚃 Process

## B1 User Flow & Competitive Analysis

Before you can fix something, you need to understand it.

**User Flow:** Map the current experience. Document every screen, every tap, every decision point in the flow you're redesigning. Note where users might get confused, frustrated, or give up.

**Competitive Analysis:** Find 2-3 apps that solve similar problems. What do they do better? What do they do worse? Create a comparison matrix.

Turn in a PDF to Learning Suite (due Day 5, end of class)


## B2 Paper Prototype V1 + User Task Diagram

Sketch your redesign on paper. Don't open Figma yet — paper forces you to focus on structure, not pixels.

**Paper Prototype:** Draw your redesigned screens. Include enough detail that someone could "use" the prototype by pointing at buttons while you flip pages.

**User Task Diagram:** Map the ideal flow. Show every step the user takes to complete their goal.

Test your paper prototype with at least 1 person (not a classmate). Note what confused them.

Turn in by 11:59pm on Learning Suite (Day 6)


## B3 Paper Prototype V2 + Testing Notes

Revise based on your first test. Test again with a different person.

**Include:**
- Revised paper prototype
- Testing notes from both sessions
- List of changes made based on feedback

Turn in PDF to Learning Suite (due Day 7)


# 🚄 B4 Final Deliverable

**Digital Prototype in Figma** showing your redesigned flow (minimum 5 screens, fully clickable).

**Process Documentation (PDF):**
- Original app analysis (what's broken and why)
- Competitive analysis matrix
- Paper prototype evolution (V1 → V2 with annotations)
- Usability testing findings (at least 2 sessions)
- Before/after comparisons of key screens
- 1-2 sentences explaining your core insight

The final prototype should feel native to your chosen platform. Interactions should work as users expect.
