# Unit B: One More Thing
## Product Design as Discovery and Problem Solving

You've created something from scratch. Now it's time to make something *better*. In this unit, you'll take an existing app — one that already works, sort of — and redesign it to actually serve its users.

### The Big Idea

Improving an existing product requires both **forming better intent** (discovering what's actually broken and for whom) and **rendering that intent** (executing improvements with appropriate craft).

The catch? Before you can fix something, you need to understand why it was designed that way in the first place. Every confusing button, every buried feature, every frustrating flow — someone made those choices. Your job is to figure out which choices were wrong, which were right for a different context, and which you can genuinely improve.

### What You'll Learn

**Good design is often invisible.** Users don't notice when things work — they notice friction, confusion, and frustration. You'll learn to see your designs through users' eyes, not your own.

**Platform conventions exist for a reason.** iOS, Android, and web users have learned to expect certain patterns. Breaking conventions has real costs. You'll learn when to follow the rules and when breaking them genuinely serves users.

**Testing reveals what you can't see alone.** Watching real people struggle with your design is uncomfortable — and essential. Usability testing isn't a phase you do at the end; it's how you discover what actually needs fixing.

**Prototyping is thinking made visible.** From paper sketches to Figma prototypes, you'll learn to work at different levels of fidelity — fast and rough when exploring, polished when validating.

### What You'll Make

A redesigned version of an existing app, progressing from competitive analysis through paper prototypes to a functional Figma prototype. You'll document why the original design fell short, what you learned from testing, and how your solution addresses real user needs.

### Why This Matters

Most design work isn't creating from nothing — it's improving what exists. Whether you're on a product team iterating on features or a consultant auditing someone else's work, the skills you build here (analysis, testing, rapid prototyping, justifying decisions) are what employers actually hire for.
