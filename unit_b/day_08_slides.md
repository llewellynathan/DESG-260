### Slide 7: The Six Sections
**B4 Documentation PDF — Six Required Sections**

1. **Title Page with Creative Brief** — Goal of the new interaction
2. **User Flow of Existing App** — With critique (pros/cons)
3. **Competitive Analysis** — Matrix + quadrant positioning chart
4. **User Task Diagrams** — Existing flow + modified flow with visual separator
5. **Paper Prototype + Iterations** — Show feedback, iterations, reasoning
6. **High-Fidelity Final Prototype** — Device mock-ups, annotations, indistinguishable from native

> The documentation itself should be well-designed. Clear typographic hierarchy, consistent grids, cohesive layout. The documentation illuminates the design — it does not clash with nor overshadow it.

---

### Slide 8: Documentation Tips
**Making Your Documentation Shine**
- Show 1-2 examples from previous semesters
- **Typographic hierarchy:** Use consistent heading sizes, weights, and spacing to guide the reader
- **Exporting from Figma:** Export frames as PNG or PDF; use Figma's built-in export settings for high resolution
- **Device mock-ups:** Place final screens into realistic device frames — Figma has plugins for this
- **Annotations:** Write concise labels that explain *why* you made a design decision, not just *what* is on screen

---

### Slide 9: Telling the Story of Your Process
**Paper Prototype + Iterations**
- Don't just show the final result — show the journey
- Include the feedback you received and how it changed your design
- Show before/after: What did the prototype look like before testing vs. after?
- Explain your reasoning: Why did you act on some feedback and not others?

> What feedback from testing did you act on? What did you decide not to change, and why?

---

### Slide 10: The Final Prototype
**High-Fidelity Final Prototype**
- Your new feature should be **indistinguishable from the original app**
- Match typography, spacing, color, and interaction patterns exactly
- Use device mock-ups to present screens in context
- Add annotations that explain your design decisions

> How do you know when a design is "done enough"? What do you polish vs. what do you cut?
