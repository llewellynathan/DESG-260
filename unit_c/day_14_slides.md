### Slide 8: What is Gestalt?
**Gestalt Psychology**
- Our brains organize visual elements into meaningful patterns
- We perceive relationships, not just individual objects
- These principles explain *why* certain design choices work

> Named from the German word for "form" or "shape" — developed by German psychologists in the 1920s

---

### Slide 9: Proximity
**Proximity**
Elements close together are perceived as related

[Visual: Two dot grids side-by-side]
- Left: Evenly spaced dots = one group
- Right: Gap in middle = two groups

→ *This is why spacing matters in your designs*

---

### Slide 10: Similarity
**Similarity**
Elements that look alike are perceived as related

[Visual: Three shape grids]
- All circles = no pattern
- Alternating circles/squares by column = columns emerge
- Alternating by row = rows emerge

→ *This is why consistency matters in your designs*

---

### Slide 11: Continuity
**Continuity**
Elements in a line or curve are perceived as connected

[Visual: Two dot arrangements]
- Smooth curve = feels connected, our eye follows the path
- Random scatter = no connection, feels chaotic

→ *This is why alignment matters in your designs*

---

### Slide 12: Closure
**Closure**
We perceive incomplete shapes as complete — our brain fills in the gaps

[Visual: Three incomplete shapes]
- Circle with a gap = still perceived as a circle
- Square missing bottom = still perceived as a square
- Three pac-man shapes = triangle appears between them

→ *Simplify icons and graphics — users will fill in the rest*

---

### Slide 13: Common Region
**Common Region**
Elements within a bounded area are perceived as grouped

[Visual: Dots with and without boundaries]
- Scattered dots = just dots
- Same dots in a box = a group
- Dots in two separate boxes = two distinct groups

→ *Cards, sections, and containers create visual grouping*

---

### Slide 14: Common Fate
**Common Fate**
Elements moving in the same direction are perceived as related

[Visual: Arrows showing movement]
- All arrows pointing right = unified, feels like a system
- Arrows pointing different directions = chaotic, unrelated

→ *Animations and transitions should move elements together intentionally*

---

### Slide 15: The Perception Gap
**When polish is off, users feel friction they can't articulate.**
They won't say "your margins are inconsistent" — they'll say "something feels off" or "this looks amateurish."

Gestalt principles aren't arbitrary rules — they're based on how human perception works.

### Slide 17: What to Check
**Common issues to fix (with Gestalt context):**
- **Alignment (Continuity):** Elements not snapping to grid; inconsistent margins
- **Spacing (Proximity):** Uneven padding; cramped or floaty elements
- **Type (Similarity):** Inconsistent styles; orphans/widows; poor line length
- **Responsive:** Content that doesn't work on mobile; touch targets too small
- **Details:** Placeholder text; low-res images; missing states

> Look at your design: which of these applies to your work right now?
