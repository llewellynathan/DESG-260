# Unit C: Hello, World
## Responsive, Online, & Mobile

Welcome to web design. This unit shifts your focus from native apps to the browser — a medium with its own constraints, conventions, and creative possibilities.

### The Big Idea

The web is fundamentally different from native platforms. It's fluid, accessible, and runs on everything from phones to ultrawide monitors. **Responsive design is the art of adapting presentation without fragmenting the experience.** You're not designing two separate pages — you're designing one system that responds intelligently to its environment.

This connects directly to our forming/rendering framework:
- **Forming intent** means understanding your user's context — who they are, what device they're using, what job they're trying to accomplish.
- **Rendering intent** means crafting a visual hierarchy that guides them efficiently, regardless of screen size.

A beautifully designed desktop page that breaks on mobile fails the rendering test. A responsive layout that ignores the user's actual needs fails the forming test. Excellence requires both.

### What You'll Learn

- **Typography creates hierarchy.** Type scale, weight, and spacing are your primary tools for guiding attention. You'll build a type system before designing layouts.

- **Grids enable responsive design.** Understanding how content reflows across breakpoints is essential for modern web and product design.

- **Personas inform design decisions.** The same product can be presented differently depending on who's buying. You'll design a Product Detail Page (PDP) tailored to a specific user's needs and values.

- **Content-first design works.** Rather than forcing content into predetermined layouts, you'll let the information architecture drive your decisions.

- **Testing reveals assumptions.** A quick usability test with real people exposes blind spots faster than hours of solo iteration.

### What You'll Make

A responsive Product Detail Page (PDP) designed for both mobile and desktop. You'll start by researching a fictional character (your persona), then design a page that prioritizes the information *they* care about most. The final deliverable includes your persona sheet, type stylesheet, wireframes, and polished high-fidelity designs.

### Why This Matters

Product pages are everywhere. E-commerce, SaaS, portfolios — nearly every digital product has some version of a detail page. Learning to design responsive layouts with clear hierarchy is foundational for web and product design careers. The skills you build here — grids, type systems, responsive thinking — transfer directly to professional work.
