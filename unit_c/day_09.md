**Focus:** Responsive design adapts presentation to context — one system, many devices.

**Targeted Learning Outcomes:**
- Better Interactions & Experiences
- Critical Analysis of Form and Format
- Design Strategy

### Desired Results

**Essential Questions:**
- EQ1: How do we design for devices we've never seen?
- EQ2: What makes a layout "responsive" rather than just "resized"?
- EQ3: How does forming intent (understanding user context) inform rendering intent (visual execution)?

**Understandings:**
- U1: Responsive design is about adapting presentation, not creating separate experiences
- U2: Grids provide the structural foundation that allows content to reflow intelligently
- U3: The web has its own constraints and conventions — it's not just "app design in a browser"
- U4: Personas help us make design decisions by grounding choices in real user needs, not assumptions

**Students Will Know:**

- S2: Write a job story that captures user motivation and desired outcome
- S3: Identify and prioritize content for a PDP based on persona needs
- S4: Explain how responsive design adapts to different devices

### Activities

1. **Warm-Up: Web vs. Native Reflection** (10 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, U3]`
   - Prompt on screen: "Open a product page on your phone. Now open the same page on your laptop. What changed? What stayed the same?"
   - Students document 3 observations about how content adapts
   - Sets up the core question: what makes responsive design work?

2. **Today's Learning Goals** (5 min) `[EQ1, EQ2, EQ3, U1, U2, U3]`
   - We're starting a new unit: designing for the web
   - Key shift: from native apps (fixed dimensions) to responsive web (infinite dimensions)
   - Today's goal: understand how grids enable responsive design, and meet your new project
   - Connect to forming/rendering: *forming* = understanding who uses your product and on what device; *rendering* = building systems that adapt to that context

3. **Direct Instruction: Responsive Design Fundamentals** (15 min) `[K1, U1, U2, U3]`
   - **I do:** Explain the core concept: designing for 100,000 devices
     - Native apps have fixed dimensions; the web doesn't
     - Responsive design adapts *presentation* to context — same content, different arrangement
     - Why "pixel-perfect" thinking fails on the web
   - **I do:** Show examples of responsive PDPs
     - Same product page on mobile vs. desktop
     - What changes: layout, image sizes, navigation
     - What stays the same: content, hierarchy, brand
   - **We do:** Analyze a responsive site together
     - Resize browser window and observe what happens
     - Identify breakpoints where layout shifts
     - Discuss: what decisions did the designer make?

4. **Direct Instruction: Personas & Job-to-be-Done** (25 min) `[K3, K4, U4, S1, S2, EQ3]`
   - **What is a persona?**
     - A fictional but research-based representation of your target user
     - NOT a demographic profile — a persona captures *motivations*, *values*, and *context*
     - Why it matters: personas help you make decisions when you can't ask the user directly
     - Bad persona: "Sarah, 28, likes coffee" — too vague, doesn't inform design
     - Good persona: specific background, clear values, defined job-to-be-done
   - **The Job-to-be-Done (JTBD) framework**
     - People don't buy products — they "hire" products to do a job
     - Classic example: "People don't want a quarter-inch drill, they want a quarter-inch hole"
     - JTBD focuses on the *outcome* the user wants, not the product features
     - Job story format: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]"
     - Example: "When I'm shopping for a gift for my partner, I want to see reviews from people in similar situations, so I can feel confident this is the right choice"
   - **How to research and document a persona**
     - Background: demographics, interests, family life, daily context
     - Core values: what matters most to this person? What do they prioritize?
     - Job-to-be-done: what are they trying to accomplish? What's the situation?
     - Write 1-2 job stories that capture their motivation
   - **Show examples**
     - Walk through 2-3 persona examples (good and weak)
     - Discuss: how would each persona's job story change what you prioritize on a PDP?
     - Connect to forming intent: understanding the user IS forming intent

5. **Introduce Assignment C: Responsive Product Detail Page** (15 min) `[EQ3, U1, K2, S3]`
   - Overview: Design a PDP for mobile AND desktop, tailored to a persona
   - Walk through the full project arc (C1 → C5)
   - **C1 due Feb 11:** Persona sheet + product details
     - Persona: background, core values, job-to-be-done (with job story)
     - Product: name, 3+ images, price, directions, shipping, reviews, related products, returns, dimensions
     - Optional: announcement bar, subscribe, gifting, company review replies
   - Key insight: same product, same information — but *how you prioritize it* depends on your persona's job story
   - Show character options (or explain how to create their own)
   - Show 2-3 example PDPs — discuss what each prioritizes and why

6. **Work Session: Persona Research + Content Gathering** (50 min) `[S1, S2, S3, EQ3]`
   - Students choose their character and begin research
   - Start documenting: demographics, interests, values, job-to-be-done
   - Write at least one job story for their persona
   - Begin gathering product content (images, specs, reviews)
   - Instructor circulates for 1:1 check-ins:
     - Is the persona specific enough?
     - Does the job story capture real motivation and outcome?
     - Does the product choice make sense for this person?
     - What would this persona prioritize on a product page?

**Total: 120 min**
