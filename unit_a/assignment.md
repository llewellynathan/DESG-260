
# Assignment 1: Wayfinding Map & Icons

## “Start Here”

“Start Here” is often used as a visual cue on a map with directions. It is different from “You Are Here” style maps that can be taken in any direction. Famously, Windows has used a “Start” button on the bottom left corner of its as its primary menu interface since 1995.


## ✏️ The Assignment

Map out an event or an experience that takes place over time or space. As part of your map, create a set of between 4 to 7 icons that represent key moments in the event/experience. Tell a story with the journey.


### WAYFINDING MAP

💚 The event or experience should include emotions, ideas, or unique physical actions.
(e.g. a dream, a story, a metaphor, a metaphysical journey, etc.)
❌ It cannot simply be directions through a physical space or a traditional map. 
💚 The map can include text, but cannot solely be a typographic map.  
💚 The map and icons should be B&W (or mono-chromatic), unless color is a critical part of the story.


### ICONS

💚 At least two of your icons should represent abstract emotions or ideas. 
💚 The icons should work as a set. Cohesive icon sets are like good typefaces, based on similar strokes, proportions, and stylistic forms. They should clear unifying principles while also being different enough to distinguish easily. 
❌ The icons should be sketched by hand (digitally or with pen/pencil) at first and then vectorized. 
❌ Cannot use existing digital icon sets. Start from scratch.

## A1 Wayfinding/Experience/Journey Map Sketches 

Bring physical sketches to class for review next class period (printed if drawn digitally).

5+ Exploration Thumbnails of the experience/journey(can be smaller, rougher, representing possible ideas—each of the five should be distinct ideas, not just five variations of the same idea.) 
1 Larger image, more finessed (should be at least 8.5 x 5.5 large, aka a half sheet—large enough for someone to see from 2-3 feet away.)

At this stage, the icons are less important than exploring the big ideas of the (although you can include ideas for icons too). Your sketches could include strings of words. Don’t just think about beginning and end points, think about the connections and the journey. What makes this a good story? Make sure to encompass the whole journey. 

These are sketches, so they can be rough, but they need to also be legible to external audiences. 

Turn in a digital PDF (photo/scan) to Learning Suite (due by end of day)
PDF should two pages: pg.1 all rough thumbnails and pg. 2 more finessed image.


## A2 Icon Set Draft

Bring a physical draft of your icon set to class (and printed if drawn digitally).
Include a revised wayfinding map to reference with the icons. 

The map is less the focus on this round of critique, but still helpful for visual context. Consider the shape of the pathway, and the visual cues that order, and spatial location have for the information that you are showcasing: What coheres together? What groups are natural? What separations are critical? Are there other bits of information that would help it be more communicative?

Icons need to be drawn at least 2 inches tall (physical), or digital equivalent. Align all 5-to-7 icons on horizontal line. Be attentive to spacing, line width, and optical alignment. 

Turn in a digital PDF (photo/scan) to Learning Suite (due by end of day)
