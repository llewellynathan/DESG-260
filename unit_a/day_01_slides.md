### Slide 9: Forming vs. Rendering
- **Good design** requires both good forming and good rendering
- **Bad design** is the result of:
  - Poorly formed intent, OR
  - Bad rendering of well-formed, good intent
- Learning to form and render intent are foundational to success in this course

### Slide 12: Understandings
- Design is the forming and rendering of intent. Forming intent means discovering the right problem and envisioning the right solution. Rendering intent means executing that vision with skill and craft.
- Even a focused project like icon design requires both forming intent (what must this icon communicate, to whom, in what context?) and rendering intent (how do I execute it with visual clarity and consistency?).

### Slide 15: Principles of Great Design & Learning
**Principles of Great Design & Learning** (10 min)
- If you want to be a great designer, be a great learner. These principles apply to both.

---

### Slide 16: Principles (Part 1)
- **Feedback reveals blindspots** — External perspectives show us what we cannot see ourselves. You can either be humble and learn from feedback or hold yourself back.
- **Externalization clarifies thinking** — Explaining your design brings clarity and helps identify gaps. If you're stuck, try talking through your problem with a friend.
- **Failure is natural** — First attempts are experiments, not final answers. Failure is core to learning and improving.

---

### Slide 17: Principles (Part 2)
- **Incubation enables breakthroughs** — Your brain continues working when you step away. When stuck, take a break.
- **Iterate from low to high fidelity** — Start with simple ideas, progress to more advanced. Start with words, iterate towards visuals.
- **Teaching deepens understanding** — You don't fully understand until you can teach it.
- **Reflection accelerates growth** — Without reflection, experience doesn't become learning.
