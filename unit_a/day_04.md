# Day 4 — Wednesday, Jan 21
## Unit A: Wayfinding Map & Icons

### Focus
How do constraints enable creativity?

---

### Desired Results

**Essential Questions:**
- EQ1: How do constraints enable creativity? Why do limitations (device size, platform guidelines, accessibility requirements) often lead to better solutions?

**Understandings:**
- U1: A cohesive visual system requires establishing and following consistent rules (stroke weight, grid, style).
- U2: Effective wayfinding requires understanding how people navigate physical and mental spaces.

**Students Will Know:**
- K1: Principles of icon design (simplicity, recognizability, consistency)
- K2: How to use vector tools in Illustrator and/or Figma

**Students Will Be Able To:**
- S1: Create vectorized icons with consistent visual style
- S2: Design a simple wayfinding system

**Targeted Learning Outcomes:** Critical Analysis of Form and Format

### Activities

*Note: Day 4 follows MLK Day (no class Jan 19)*

1. **Quick Check-In: Progress Share** (10 min) `[S1, S2]`
   - Students show current vectorized icons on screen or printed
   - Identify anyone who's stuck or needs help with vector tools
   - Quick troubleshooting for common issues

2. **Constraints Discussion** (20 min) `[EQ1, U1]`
   - Frame: "How do constraints enable creativity?"
   - Discuss how the B&W limitation, icon count (4-7), and map format forced clearer decisions
   - Connect to forming/rendering: constraints sharpen both intent AND execution
   - Ask: "What constraint pushed you to a better solution than you would have found otherwise?"
   - Share examples of how professional designers embrace constraints (e.g., Dieter Rams, icon design systems)

3. **Work Session with 1:1 Meetings** (60 min) `[S1, S2, K2, U1]`
   - Students finalize vectorized icons and map design
   - Instructor meets individually (5 min each) to review progress
   - Focus feedback on:
     - Visual hierarchy: Is the journey readable?
     - Composition: How do icons integrate with the map?
     - Consistency: Are stroke weights, proportions, and style unified?
     - Type choice: Does any text support or compete with the icons?

4. **Introduce A4: Final Deliverable** (15 min) `[U2, K1]`
   - Frame: This is your first complete design case study — from problem to solution
   - **A4 requirements (due Day 5, Monday Jan 26):**
     - PDF process book showcasing sketches → finished work
     - Final map on one page (default 17" x 11" horizontal, other sizes permitted)
     - 1-2 sentences illuminating the core concept (keep it short)
     - At least 1 caption describing how you changed the design in response to feedback
   - Explain: Day 5 begins Project B, so A4 must be complete before class
   - Show example of strong process documentation (if available)
   - Discuss what makes a good concept statement: specific, not generic

5. **Peer Review: Final Polish** (20 min) `[K1, U1, S1]`
   - Partners review each other's near-final work
   - Checklist for reviewers:
     - Are icons optically aligned on the baseline?
     - Is stroke weight consistent across all icons?
     - Is the map readable from 2-3 feet away?
     - Do at least 2 icons represent abstract emotions/ideas?
     - Is there a clear beginning and end to the journey?
   - Written feedback: note 1 strength and 1 suggestion for each element

6. **Work Session: Final Push** (45 min) `[S1, S2, K2]`
   - Apply peer feedback
   - Finalize vectors and prepare A3 submission
   - Continue 1:1 meetings for students who need additional support
   - Students submit A3 to Learning Suite before leaving

7. **Wrap-up & Homework Clarification** (10 min) `[U2]`
   - Confirm A3 submissions are uploaded
   - Remind: A4 Final Deliverable due Day 5 (Monday, Jan 26) before class
   - Preview: Day 5 introduces Project B — come ready for a new challenge
   - Encourage students to review Frank Chimero reading for insights on "How and Why"

**Total time:** 180 minutes
