# Unit A: Start Here
## Journeys, Destinations, Touchpoints

Welcome to your first design project. Before diving into apps and interfaces, we're starting with something deceptively simple: a map and a set of icons.

### The Big Idea

This course is built on one central concept: **design is the forming and rendering of intent.**

- **Forming intent** means discovering the right problem and envisioning the right solution.
- **Rendering intent** means executing that vision with skill and craft.

Good design requires excellence in both. A brilliant idea poorly executed fails. A beautifully crafted solution to the wrong problem also fails. Over these two weeks, you'll practice both — deciding *what* story your map should tell, and *how* to tell it with visual clarity.

### What You'll Learn

**Experience mapping is a core UX practice.** Professional designers create journey maps, user flows, and experience maps to visualize the full picture of how people move through products and services. This skill helps you identify pain points, spot opportunities, and communicate user stories to teammates and stakeholders. You're learning a tool you'll use throughout your career.

**Icons are functional communication tools, not decoration.** They need to be instantly recognizable and work as a cohesive system. You'll learn what makes icon sets feel unified — consistent stroke weights, proportions, and style.

**Constraints enable creativity.** Working in black and white with a limited number of icons might feel restrictive. But limitations force clearer decisions and often lead to stronger solutions.

**Testing isn't separate from designing — it *is* designing.** You'll get feedback early and often, learning to improve your work based on how others actually experience it.

### What You'll Make

A wayfinding/experience map that tells the story of a journey — not just physical directions, but an experience with emotions, ideas, and key moments. You'll create 4-7 icons to mark the touchpoints along the way, then document your process from rough sketches to polished vectors.

### Why This Matters

This is your first design case study. The process book you create isn't just an assignment — it's the beginning of how you'll document and present your work as a designer. The habits you build here (sketching, testing, iterating, reflecting) are the same habits that define professional practice.
