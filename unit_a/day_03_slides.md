### Slide 6: Critique Round 1
**Functional/Usability Critique**
Icons are functional communication tools — if someone can't understand it, the icon fails regardless of how beautiful it is

---

### Slide 7: Critique Round 1 Instructions
**Rotating silent critique**
1. Move through 3-4 peers' icon sets
2. Write feedback on sticky notes

**Guiding questions:**
- Can you identify what this represents?
- Does it communicate the intended emotion?
- Would a user understand this at small sizes?

---

### Slide 8: Critique Round 2
**Formal/Aesthetics Critique**
Cohesive icon sets are like good typefaces — similar strokes, proportions, and stylistic forms

---

### Slide 9: Critique Round 2 Instructions
**Rotating silent critique** (same format)

**Guiding questions:**
- Is this a cohesive set?
- Consistent stroke weight?
- Similar proportions?
- Do they look like they belong together?

---

### Slide 10: Icon Design Principles
**Three Principles for Effective Icons**
- **Simplicity:** Reduce to essential forms; remove unnecessary detail
- **Recognizability:** Users should identify meaning instantly
- **Consistency:** Unified stroke weight, proportions, and style across the set

Round 1 tested recognizability. Round 2 tested consistency.

---

### Slide 11: Form and Function
**What is the relationship between form and function?**

- **Function = Forming intent** — What must this communicate?
- **Form = Rendering intent** — How do I execute it beautifully?

A brilliant idea poorly executed fails. A beautifully crafted solution to the wrong problem also fails.

> Are form and function ever in tension?

### Slide 13: Learning Vector Tools
**Vectors vs. Pixels**
- Vectors scale infinitely without quality loss
- Icons need to work at many sizes — vectors are essential

**Tips for consistency:**
- Use a grid
- Set a standard stroke weight
- Align anchor points
