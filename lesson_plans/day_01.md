# Day 1 — Wednesday, Jan 7
## Unit A: Wayfinding Map & Icons

### Focus
What is design? Forming vs. rendering intent.

---

### Desired Results

**Essential Questions:**
- What is design?
- What is the difference between forming intent and rendering intent—and why does good design require excellence in both?
- How do symbols communicate meaning across language and culture?

**Understandings:**
- Design is the forming and rendering of intent. Forming intent means discovering the right problem and envisioning the right solution. Rendering intent means executing that vision with skill and craft.
- Even a focused project like icon design requires both forming intent (what must this icon communicate, to whom, in what context?) and rendering intent (how do I execute it with visual clarity and consistency?).

**Students Will Know:**
- The distinction between forming intent (discovering the right problem and envisioning the right solution) and rendering intent (executing that vision skillfully)
- The vocabulary and concepts of user experience design (affordances, signifiers, feedback, mental models, user flows)
- What makes wayfinding systems succeed or fail

**Students Will Be Able To:**
- Identify design problems worth solving through curiosity and empathy

**Targeted Learning Outcomes:** Critical Analysis of Form and Format, Better Interactions & Experiences

### Activities

1. **Welcome & Introductions** (10 min)
   - Brief personal introductions (name, year, one thing you designed recently)
   - Set expectations for studio culture: critique, iteration, curiosity

2. **Syllabus & Course Overview** (10 min)
   - Walk through four major projects (A-D) and how they build on each other
   - Explain forming vs. rendering intent as the course's central framework
   - Point to syllabus for policies; don't read it aloud

3. **"What is Design?" Discussion** (15 min)
   - Ask: What is an interface? A designer? Usability? User experience?
   - Introduce forming vs. rendering framework as answer to "What is design?"
   - Key vocabulary: affordances, signifiers, feedback, mental models

4. **Wayfinding Examples Gallery Walk** (15 min)
   - Show 5-6 examples (traditional maps, emotional journeys, metaphorical maps, user flows)
   - Students identify: What makes each effective/ineffective?
   - Discuss: Mental model vs. conceptual model — what does the user expect vs. what the designer intended?
   - Ask: How do symbols communicate meaning across language and culture? What symbols are universal vs. culturally specific?

5. **Introduce Assignment 1: Wayfinding Map & Icons** (20 min)
   - Frame the project using forming/rendering:
     - **Forming intent:** What journey will you map? What story will you tell? What must your icons communicate, to whom, in what context?
     - **Rendering intent:** How will you execute it with visual clarity and consistency?
   - Walk through full assignment (A1-A4) so students see the arc:
     - A1: Sketches (due Day 2)
     - A2: Icon Set Draft (due Day 3)
     - A3: Vectorized Icons (due Day 4)
     - A4: Final Process Book (due Day 5)
   - **Focus on A1 requirements:**
     - 5+ exploration thumbnails (distinct ideas, not variations)
     - 1 larger, more finessed sketch (at least 8.5 x 5.5)
     - Physical sketches to bring to class (printed if digital)
   - Clarify constraints: B&W, no existing icon sets, abstract emotions required
   - **Due: Day 2 (Monday, Jan 12) — bring physical sketches to class**
   - Explain: sketches will be reviewed and tested by peers

6. **Brainstorming: Identify Problems Worth Solving** (15 min)
   - Frame: Good design starts with curiosity and empathy — what journeys do people struggle to navigate? What experiences are confusing or emotionally complex?
   - Students generate 5+ distinct journey ideas (dreams, stories, metaphors, processes)
   - Emphasize: NOT just physical directions — think about user flows through experiences
   - Quick share with neighbor: What problem does your journey address? Who would benefit from this map?
   - This is the first step of A1 — continue refining ideas as homework

**Total time:** 85 minutes
