## What the final deliverable looks like for Assignment C:

    1. You’ll be turning in a PDF of your Product Detail Page (PDP) formatted for desktop and mobile viewing. 

### How to do this project:

1. You will be given a character and their “job to be done.”

2.  Research your character.
What do they like? What do they dislike? What is important to them? You’ll also be extrapolating a bit on what this character would do in a scenario like this. (Are they more willing to spend money on a quality item, or are they more interested in a good deal?)

    [ ] Write a small paragraph describing who they are as a character (demographic info as well as interests, and additional context you find relevant
    [ ] Write a list of values that are important to them

3. Taking the information provided below, organize it into a Product Detail Page (PDP) in a way that it would suit your character.
    
4. Create a stylesheet 
    Do they need more or less information? Do they care about free shipping? Returns? Gifting options? Do they prefer to be guided, sort through pre-established recommendations, or do they want a high level of customization?

    This process could look like the following: 
    a. Rough sketches to outline where your pieces of information could go, all the while considering what your character needs/cares about the most. 
    b. Start to block in information with rough digital wireframes. 
    c. Finesse into final high fidelity page


5. Turn in a final high fidelity Product Detail Page
    1. PDP dimensions choose the frame size that matches your phone width and extend it however long you need to make your page. 
    2. Reminder—you do NOT need to create a page footer, or the navigation bar for this assignment

## How to know if you did it right:

1. Did you include all the required information on your page?
2. Is your page clear and easy to navigate?
3. Is your page built off a strong grid system, and do your elements align to it?
4. Does your page exhibit thoughtful layout informed by your assigned character’s needs and  “job to be done?”

## Characters 

I want to emphasize that the PDP will not have to look like the character you are given in terms of design (i.e. Dora’s page won’t have to look cartoon-y and be all orange and purple.) These characters are simply a way for us to narrow down an experience that a user could be looking for as we approach our page hierarchy.  A very specific persona, if you will.
